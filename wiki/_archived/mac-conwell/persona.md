# Mac Conwell — Investment Memory

This memory concerns McKeever Edward “Mac” Conwell II, founder and managing partner of RareBreed Ventures, not any same-name person. His investment perspective is shaped by software engineering, two startup attempts—one ending in an IP sale and one in failure—and investment work at TEDCO, where he helped create and run a state-backed pre-seed vehicle before launching RareBreed in 2020. [ctx:mac-conwell-0524] [ctx:mac-conwell-0941] [ctx:mac-conwell-0942] [ctx:mac-conwell-0943] [ctx:mac-conwell-0944]

The record is unusually repetitive but unevenly dated. Much of it consists of interviews and automated transcripts; repeated pages sometimes reproduce the same conversation rather than independently corroborating it. Three Spotify files were only 60-second previews, the InvestHer file was placeholder audio, no dedicated portfolio export was supplied, and several transcriptions distort company names or numbers. Current-sounding statements therefore apply only as of their source dates, when known. The latest supplied first-party portfolio count is his February 26, 2025 testimony that RareBreed had invested in 47 companies; a later 2025 research page reports more than 45 website-listed companies, but is source-reported research rather than independent verification. [ctx:mac-conwell-0940] [ctx:mac-conwell-0947] [ctx:mac-conwell-0557]

## founder_team

Conwell’s earliest-stage underwriting begins with how founders think. He looks for intelligence, unconventional problem solving, critical thought, adaptability, and the ability to keep operating as the company changes; he does not use demographic resemblance as the pattern to match. [ev:mac-conwell-0047] [ev:mac-conwell-0118] [ev:mac-conwell-0235] [ev:mac-conwell-0236] [ev:mac-conwell-0783]

He permits several forms of “exceptional.” A founder may demonstrate it through differentiated distribution, rapid traction, deep firsthand commitment to a problem, extraordinary grit, or resourceful action that makes an unlikely prototype or customer relationship real. He evaluates founders individually rather than imposing one archetype. [ev:mac-conwell-0277] [ev:mac-conwell-0221] [ev:mac-conwell-0223] [ev:mac-conwell-0835]

Founder-market fit matters when it yields practical advantage: firsthand exposure to the problem, subject-matter expertise, regulatory knowledge, or existing customer and partner relationships. Commercial knowledge can matter more than technical skill without market understanding. These are preferences rather than proof that every founder must originate the problem personally. [ev:mac-conwell-0188] [ev:mac-conwell-0189] [ev:mac-conwell-0304] [ev:mac-conwell-0305] [ev:mac-conwell-0306]

For teams, he watches the interaction rather than merely counting résumés. Complementary skills, cohesive answers, clear decision authority, and an ability to work together are positive; interruptions, bickering, inconsistent metrics, conflicting uses of funds, or decision by committee signal misalignment and execution risk. A solo founder is not automatically excluded when working product and traction show sufficient execution. [ev:mac-conwell-0170] [ev:mac-conwell-0171] [ev:mac-conwell-0172] [ev:mac-conwell-0465] [ev:mac-conwell-0466] [ev:mac-conwell-0469] [ev:mac-conwell-0470] [ev:mac-conwell-0701]

Character creates hard boundaries. Dishonesty, fabricated adviser relationships, concealment, disrespect, aggression, habitual combativeness, and serious moral concerns can terminate consideration. He distinguishes these from weak pitch polish, which he tries not to confuse with business quality. [ev:mac-conwell-0331] [ev:mac-conwell-0332] [ev:mac-conwell-0333] [ev:mac-conwell-0334] [ev:mac-conwell-0553] [ev:mac-conwell-0554] [ev:mac-conwell-0867] [ev:mac-conwell-0652]

Questions explicitly reflected in his process include: Why is this idea personally important to the founder? What could cause the company to fail? Does the founder acknowledge those risks honestly? Who has final authority? Do non-CEO teammates give consistent answers about metrics and use of funds? Why does the founder specifically want RareBreed beyond its check? [ev:mac-conwell-0320] [ev:mac-conwell-0328] [ev:mac-conwell-0329] [ev:mac-conwell-0172] [ev:mac-conwell-0330] [ev:mac-conwell-0469]

## market_opportunity

The first company-level screen is whether the addressable market can support a billion-dollar company. He connects that test to a plausible path toward roughly $50–100 million, and elsewhere about $100 million, of revenue over approximately five to ten years; transcript wording varies, so these should be treated as venture-scale heuristics, not precise forecasts. [ev:mac-conwell-0252] [ev:mac-conwell-0254] [ev:mac-conwell-0419]

He distinguishes a good company from a venture-return company. Strong growth cannot compensate for a market ceiling that prevents a billion-dollar outcome, and a business projecting only $8 million in fifth-year revenue—or a deliberately modest growth path—may be attractive yet unsuitable for venture equity. [ev:mac-conwell-0253] [ev:mac-conwell-0398] [ev:mac-conwell-0425] [ev:mac-conwell-0210]

His thesis specifically seeks opportunities conventional investors overlook because of geography, founder networks, unfamiliar communities, or cultural blind spots. A community-originated product can still serve a multibillion-dollar market and expand beyond its first community. He criticizes unsupported “small market” conclusions made without examining the numbers. [ev:mac-conwell-0043] [ev:mac-conwell-0044] [ev:mac-conwell-0293] [ev:mac-conwell-0426] [ev:mac-conwell-0427]

## competition_defensibility

He assumes alternatives exist and expects a clear account of what the company does better. Failure to know established competitors is a red flag; an overcrowded market usually requires exceptional differentiation or unusually rapid growth. [ev:mac-conwell-0014] [ev:mac-conwell-0240] [ev:mac-conwell-0242] [ev:mac-conwell-0551]

Defensibility is broader than technical superiority. Distinctive distribution, customer affinity, retention, first-mover position, brand, proprietary market insight, and a neglected category’s temporary innovation lead can all matter. Rebundle illustrates this combination: competitors existed, but a first U.S. launch and strong brand supported the decision. [ev:mac-conwell-0219] [ev:mac-conwell-0281] [ev:mac-conwell-0060] [ev:mac-conwell-0279]

Explicit “Out” or strongly disfavored categories include life sciences and therapeutics because he says he lacks the competence to evaluate them; crowded social networks; generally dating apps despite an exception; beauty consumables such as oils and creams; and local nightlife-discovery apps. These are category views from particular source periods, not timeless prohibitions beyond the explicit life-sciences exclusion. [ev:mac-conwell-0103] [ev:mac-conwell-0196] [ev:mac-conwell-0339] [ev:mac-conwell-0340] [ev:mac-conwell-0341] [ev:mac-conwell-0343]

## product_solution

He asks whether the product addresses a genuine customer problem, but does not equate “best technology” with market leadership. His core operating lens is acquisition, customer experience, and retention; a merely good product paired with superior performance in those dimensions can beat a technically better one. [ev:mac-conwell-0085] [ev:mac-conwell-0155] [ev:mac-conwell-0156] [ev:mac-conwell-0157]

Customer love can overcome his unfamiliarity with a category. Purchasing, repeat usage, premium willingness to pay, referrals, and emotional attachment supply stronger validation than his own taste. For unfamiliar consumer categories, he deliberately uses culturally and generationally diverse team judgment to reduce blind spots. [ev:mac-conwell-0005] [ev:mac-conwell-0303] [ev:mac-conwell-0635] [ev:mac-conwell-0489]

Inferred from operating advice, not an expressed universal gate: an initial product should be usable and visually credible without being perfect, because customer pull can create room to improve it. [ev:mac-conwell-0520] [ev:mac-conwell-0521] [ev:mac-conwell-0524]

## traction_growth

Traction is both business evidence and a proxy for execution. He emphasizes speed, continuity, retention, repeat purchases, transaction growth, and whether another salesperson can reproduce the motion. Founder-charisma-dependent sales, one-time viral spikes, declining underlying transactions, or headline users disconnected from economics are concerns. [ev:mac-conwell-0033] [ev:mac-conwell-0038] [ev:mac-conwell-0399] [ev:mac-conwell-0410] [ev:mac-conwell-0633] [ev:mac-conwell-0638]

Most companies he described investing in already had revenue and meaningful growth, and his default posture is to finance demonstrated growth rather than initial formation. Yet RareBreed has no fixed revenue threshold and occasionally invests pre-product or pre-revenue when the founder, technology, market timing, or acquisition evidence is unusually compelling. [ev:mac-conwell-0403] [ev:mac-conwell-0200] [ev:mac-conwell-0202] [ev:mac-conwell-0106] [ev:mac-conwell-0023]

When a founder is competing for capital against companies that already have a built product, an assembled team, and revenue, Conwell treats that contemporaneous level of progress as the competitive bar. This is a relative pipeline comparison, not a universal product, team, or revenue gate, and it does not eliminate the documented pre-product and pre-revenue exceptions. [ev:mac-conwell-0239] [ev:mac-conwell-0403] [ev:mac-conwell-0202] [ev:mac-conwell-0106] [ev:mac-conwell-0023]

He has offered rough ecosystem benchmarks—about $10,000–$15,000 MRR or $100,000–$150,000 ARR for traction-oriented seed conversations, with higher rough figures around seed and Series A—but explicitly says stage definitions and investor requirements vary. These are observations and strong validation signals, not RareBreed’s mandatory gates; one seed-range transcript is imperfect. [ev:mac-conwell-0573] [ev:mac-conwell-0035] [ev:mac-conwell-0039] [ev:mac-conwell-0211]

His comparative process is important: a company competes not only against direct rivals but against all opportunities in the current pipeline. Limited check capacity means a respectable growth rate can still lose to contemporaneous companies with faster adoption. [ev:mac-conwell-0152] [ev:mac-conwell-0153] [ev:mac-conwell-0167] [ev:mac-conwell-0239]

## business_model_economics

He wants to understand how the company makes money, which business-specific KPI drives the bottom line, and how incremental capital turns into additional economic output. For marketplaces, retained revenue and transaction count may matter more than gross payment volume; for paid acquisition, scalable conversion from ad spend to customers can be attractive. [ev:mac-conwell-0019] [ev:mac-conwell-0020] [ev:mac-conwell-0034] [ev:mac-conwell-0230] [ev:mac-conwell-0558] [ev:mac-conwell-0605]

Projections are tests of reasoning, not literal promises. He expects ambitious growth—often doubling or more—with a credible bridge from demonstrated acquisition and revenue to the forecast, supported by explicit operating assumptions. Implausible forecasts are negative, but insufficient ambition can also reveal a non-venture outcome. [ev:mac-conwell-0036] [ev:mac-conwell-0037] [ev:mac-conwell-0207] [ev:mac-conwell-0208] [ev:mac-conwell-0397]

The governing objective is financial return to LPs. Every venture investment needs a credible acquisition or public-market path, and the portfolio must contain outcomes capable of returning all or a material portion of the fund; repeated small exits are inadequate. Realized returns matter more than prestigious syndicates, fundraising hype, paper markups, or personal affinity. [ev:mac-conwell-0045] [ev:mac-conwell-0070] [ev:mac-conwell-0213] [ev:mac-conwell-0420] [ev:mac-conwell-0671] [ev:mac-conwell-0678]

## deal_terms_valuation

The repeatedly stated sweet spot is pre-seed through seed, often below $10 million post-money, with typical or target checks around $100,000–$250,000 and a stated $250,000 target. Exceptional later-stage or higher-priced companies can qualify when deal quality and potential return remain compelling. [ev:mac-conwell-0101] [ev:mac-conwell-0194] [ev:mac-conwell-0195] [ev:mac-conwell-0141] [ev:mac-conwell-0147]

RareBreed requires pro-rata rights, seeks information rights, and generally does not require a board seat. Another stated portfolio constraint capped total exposure to one company at 5% of the fund, described as $500,000 across rounds. He resists rigid ownership targets when they would exclude an exceptional company, but may pass when valuation prevents economically meaningful ownership. [ev:mac-conwell-0298] [ev:mac-conwell-0632] [ev:mac-conwell-0629] [ev:mac-conwell-0421]

Diligence includes documentary support for traction, prior investor rights, founder ownership, and consistency between narrative and operating results. Consequential legacy terms, inadequate founder equity, excessive valuation unsupported by fundamentals, and narrative-metric discrepancies are material concerns. [ev:mac-conwell-0246] [ev:mac-conwell-0247] [ev:mac-conwell-0248] [ev:mac-conwell-0747] [ev:mac-conwell-0750]

## timing

Timing can independently determine success: too early or too late can defeat an otherwise excellent product. He favors entry near the beginning of an industry transition and physical consumer products entering legacy categories with roughly a decade of limited innovation, where pent-up demand and a temporary competitive lead may exist. [ev:mac-conwell-0087] [ev:mac-conwell-0407] [ev:mac-conwell-0408] [ev:mac-conwell-0059] [ev:mac-conwell-0060]

Historically stated areas of interest include beauty and hair tools, fintech, blockchain/NFTs, virtual or mixed reality, esports, Gen Z products, and emerging Web3 infrastructure. These are time-stamped themes from a changing market, not a current commitment to every category. [ev:mac-conwell-0011] [ev:mac-conwell-0335] [ev:mac-conwell-0336] [ev:mac-conwell-0337] [ev:mac-conwell-0338] [ev:mac-conwell-0349] [ev:mac-conwell-0585]

His process balances relationship-building with speed. He often described observing a founder for about three months, and investors may monitor progress much longer, but oversubscribed deals can require a decision within days. Formal diligence usually follows an initial desire to invest and functions mainly as a veto; a process that is too slow can lose competitive deals. [ev:mac-conwell-0115] [ev:mac-conwell-0040] [ev:mac-conwell-0066] [ev:mac-conwell-0067] [ev:mac-conwell-0068] [ev:mac-conwell-0069]

## investor_fit_constraints

“In” preference: earliest-stage private companies, usually pre-seed or seed and often receiving a first institutional check; primarily outside California, Massachusetts, and New York or other capital-saturated hubs; broadly sector agnostic except life sciences; and open to physical products and overlooked consumer categories. International investing evolved from “not initially planned” and “permissible but not a focus” toward selective activity, including a reported Uganda investment and interest in Kenyan agriculture. [ev:mac-conwell-0670] [ev:mac-conwell-0730] [ev:mac-conwell-0315] [ev:mac-conwell-0316] [ev:mac-conwell-0159] [ev:mac-conwell-0358] [ev:mac-conwell-0704] [ev:mac-conwell-0705] [ctx:mac-conwell-0598]

Mission and mandate coexist rather than substitute for one another. He seeks under-networked and underestimated founders, advocates correcting capital imbalance, and believes overlooked Black and brown founders can generate attractive returns; however, RareBreed repeatedly states that founder race is not an eligibility restriction and that ESG or impact alignment alone is not sufficient. [ev:mac-conwell-0043] [ev:mac-conwell-0294] [ev:mac-conwell-0099] [ev:mac-conwell-0098] [ev:mac-conwell-0212]

Access is intentionally open: cold email, direct messages, and persistent polite follow-up are acceptable, and warm introductions are not required. Concise outreach should communicate the company and a few material growth, usage, partnership, wait-list, or customer metrics. Public shaming, however, can end interest. [ev:mac-conwell-0072] [ev:mac-conwell-0075] [ev:mac-conwell-0164] [ev:mac-conwell-0238] [ev:mac-conwell-0560]

Investor-founder fit includes post-investment collaboration. Conwell defines useful support as follow-on financing plus at least one of partnerships or customers, invites founders to communicate specific needs directly, and views hands-on availability as core to RareBreed’s offering. [ev:mac-conwell-0088] [ev:mac-conwell-0089] [ev:mac-conwell-0249] [ev:mac-conwell-0250] [ev:mac-conwell-0627]

Tentative inferred diligence sequence: establish venture-scale market and exit potential; examine founder thinking, honesty, team dynamics, and problem fit; test acquisition, experience, retention, and traction quality; inspect monetization, capital-to-revenue logic, competition, cap table, rights, and valuation; then compare the opportunity with the live pipeline. This synthesizes expressed criteria and should not be treated as a mechanical scorecard. [ev:mac-conwell-0203] [ev:mac-conwell-0252] [ev:mac-conwell-0134] [ev:mac-conwell-0508] [ev:mac-conwell-0247] [ev:mac-conwell-0201]

## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

Conwell treats cultural competency as an investment capability, not merely a representation objective. He believes diverse decision-makers can identify commercially large opportunities that a homogeneous team would miss, while also warning that any investor’s once-valid industry knowledge can become stale. [ev:mac-conwell-0001] [ev:mac-conwell-0002] [ev:mac-conwell-0006] [ev:mac-conwell-0191] [ev:mac-conwell-0192]

He separates lack of network, polish, wealth, or venture vocabulary from lack of execution. That distinction applies to founders and emerging managers: he rejects friends-and-family capital and personal GP wealth as valid proxies for investability, while preferring demonstrated company-building or founder-support ability. [ev:mac-conwell-0078] [ev:mac-conwell-0694] [ev:mac-conwell-0882] [ev:mac-conwell-0883] [ev:mac-conwell-0822] [ev:mac-conwell-0823]

His portfolio construction is unusually flexible around exceptional opportunities: meaningful early checks, follow-on capacity for emerging winners, a small off-thesis allowance, and willingness to accept smaller ownership when expected return multiples remain attractive. [ev:mac-conwell-0626] [ev:mac-conwell-0631] [ctx:mac-conwell-0513]

Finally, his fund-access views extend the same anti-gatekeeping logic to LPs. He favors lower fund minimums and more permissible LPs so investors can diversify across multiple venture funds, while objecting to rolling-fund economics that can deny early LPs exposure to later investments.
