# Mark Phillips — Recurring Investment Theses

## Direct venture investment

- **Founder and business outcomes are co-equal design objectives, explicit and persistent.** From early interviews through later annual letters, Phillips describes founder wellbeing as the bedrock of 11 Tribes’ thesis rather than an incidental portfolio service. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0005] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0269] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0270]

- **Founder resilience is investable risk reduction, explicit.** The firm’s 2% non-dilutive allocation supports coaching, counseling, leadership development, and organizational health; Phillips expects this to mitigate co-founder conflict and other people-related failures. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0158] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0120] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0347]

- **Capital-efficient “enduring companies,” explicit.** The preferred company sits between lifestyle and hypergrowth: scalable and ambitious, but able to reach profitability with properly sized capital and avoid serial financing as a default. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0135] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0309] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0344] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0345]

- **Price discipline creates the moderate-exit model, explicit.** Entry valuation must leave room for roughly 10x underwriting and attractive outcomes around $50–300 million; undisciplined entry multiples make otherwise successful moderate exits inadequate. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0249] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0250] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0222]

- **Overlooked geographies provide price arbitrage, explicit.** Phillips targets non-coastal North American ecosystems where he believes comparable founder quality can be purchased at lower seed valuations. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0197] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0198] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0357]

- **Operator-founders in niches, explicit.** Deep industry experience, a unique right to win, capital efficiency, and uncrowded markets form a recurring combination. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0291] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0126]

- **Small markets can be strategically attractive, explicit.** A bounded market may reduce competitive intensity and offer a larger attainable share, provided customer pain, economics, and exit scale fit the fund. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0312] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0185]

- **Vertical AI rather than generic AI, explicit and newer.** AI wrappers and foundational LLMs are excluded, while applied vertical AI is attractive when domain expertise, distribution, workflow integration, and measurable customer value provide defensibility. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0131] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0245] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0246]

- **Distribution is replacing product as moat, explicit.** Cheap code weakens product differentiation; trusted customer access, workflow embedding, community, and superior data-learning systems become more important. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0139] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0141] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0276]

- **Purpose and human flourishing, explicit but not religiously exclusive.** He favors businesses improving employees and customer communities and excludes vice, but does not require a statement of faith. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0028] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0030] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0032] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0023]

- **Inferred from operating advice:** self-awareness, resilience, rest, low ego, active listening, decisive action, and multidimensional identity appear to inform founder fit, but most such remarks are leadership counsel rather than stated universal investment screens. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0092] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0233] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0322] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0325]

## Evolution and tensions

- **Stage tightened over time.** Early Fund I included pre-seed and pre-revenue deals; later Fund II was described as generally or exclusively post-revenue, normally around $500,000–$2 million of revenue. Exceptional contracted demand can justify underwriting pre-revenue risk. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0069] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0124] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0129] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0168]

- **Check size and leadership changed with fund scale.** Fund I sources cite approximately $250,000 checks and minority/follow-on participation; Fund II sources cite $1–1.5 million checks, while a 2026 first-party account says the firm leads virtually every round. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0107] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0127] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0138] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0310]

- **Generalist mandate, changing thematic emphasis.** Early themes included future of work, education, agriculture, and food traceability; later emphasis moved toward vertical AI and operator-built niche solutions. The firm remains sector-agnostic subject to express exclusions and concentration limits. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0201] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0192] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0102]

- **Not anti-unicorn, but anti-required-unicorn.** Phillips acknowledges that taking less capital can cap upside and says billion-dollar outcomes are welcome; the objection is forcing that path where disciplined entry pricing and a moderate exit could already produce exceptional returns. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0060] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0305]

- **Not anti-financing.** Substantial financing is not inherently disqualifying, but it is a risk and usually sits outside the preferred capital-efficient model. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0072] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0124]

## Fund allocation strategy

- **Small, concentrated, hands-on funds.** Fund I was an $11 million vehicle; Fund II closed at approximately $46–46.4 million, targeting roughly 25–30 companies and $1–1.5 million checks. A later first-party strategy says 20–25 companies, reflecting either refinement or fund-specific variation. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0040] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0316] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0127] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0306]

- **Follow-on reserves and winner concentration.** An earlier Fund I allocation reserved roughly 55–60% for follow-ons, and Phillips favors increasing exposure to companies demonstrating winning trajectories. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0106] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0359]

- **Modeled portfolio outcome.** He has described seeking a 30–40% success rate with $100–150 million exits rather than a pure power-law dependency on one unicorn. This is a stated model, not verified performance. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0061]

- **LP network as portfolio infrastructure.** Phillips views an operator-heavy LP base, subject-matter experts, and shared carry as sources of operating value, introductions, and differentiated support. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0090] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0216] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0271]

## Other asset classes and strategies

The supplied record does not establish a recurring Phillips thesis for public equities or real estate. References to markets concern venture deployment, and nearly all documented direct investments are attributed to 11 Tribes or its funds rather than a separate personal portfolio. The Pitch reports two show-related investments totaling $255,000, but the source does not establish whether those were personal or fund investments. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0283]

- **Operating-company strategy, distinct from investment policy:** Phillips advises founders to prioritize customers and cash, build from direct customer feedback, simplify structure, define multidimensional success, and preserve equity unless capital is necessary. These are operating or financing recommendations and should not automatically be treated as universal investment gates.
