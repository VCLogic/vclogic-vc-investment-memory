## Mandate and constraints

- **Vehicle and allocation:** Hyatt says he has no venture fund and invests through a family office involving himself, Richard, and partners. Sources describe public equities, real estate, funds, private equity/venture, direct investments, acquisitions, and major operating-company stakes; the mix has been described differently over time rather than as a fixed permanent policy. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0008] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0009] [ev:michael-hyatt-0094] [ev:michael-hyatt-0277]

- **Preservation objective:** As of the 2022 CNBC reporting, the office targeted roughly equal allocations to stocks, real estate, and venture/private equity and an 8% annual return; Markets Group separately reports thirds allocated to public equities, private equity strategies, and late-stage venture. These source-specific descriptions do not establish a current mandate or a reliable chronology of allocation changes. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0101] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0188] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0447] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0448]

- **Direct-investment concentration:** He can wait because he has no deployment obligation; he favors a few meaningful, actively supported positions and assesses whether the operational burden is worth the prospective return and downside. [ev:michael-hyatt-0022] [ev:michael-hyatt-0070] [ev:michael-hyatt-0268] [ev:michael-hyatt-0270] [ev:michael-hyatt-0271]

- **Control preference:** He personally prefers private-equity structures with larger ownership and control, because powerless minority positions are unattractive when execution deteriorates. DataStealth is the clearest documented control transaction. [ev:michael-hyatt-0098] [ev:michael-hyatt-0099] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0227]

- **Stage:** His personal direct activity spans startup investing and a majority acquisition, while Northleaf work focuses on larger, later-stage, more profitable companies. No single stage mandate should be inferred across vehicles. [ev:michael-hyatt-0096] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0093] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0107]

- **Sector boundaries:** Direct evidence favors B2B technology, enterprise software, cloud, security, and substantive AI, while excluding clean tech and health tech from his own competence set. Aggregator claims of very broad sector coverage are lower-confidence and conflict with this stated boundary. [ev:michael-hyatt-0009] [ev:michael-hyatt-0138] [ev:michael-hyatt-0279] [ev:michael-hyatt-0280] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0335]

- **Geography:** His operating history and many relationships are Canadian, with a strong view that Canadian companies should orient toward the US market. Aggregator claims of broad international coverage are not independently verified. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0032] [ev:michael-hyatt-0119] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0336]

- **Check size:** No dependable universal range is established. The Pitch documents one $50,000 SAFE, while aggregators conflict between $5,000–$50,000 and $1 million–$50 million; the majority DataStealth price was undisclosed. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0175] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0126] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0116] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0229]

- **Governance and co-investment:** He may invest directly but prefers useful VC co-investors for diligence, aligned terms, board oversight, and durable shareholder protection. [ev:michael-hyatt-0021] [ev:michael-hyatt-0390] [ev:michael-hyatt-0391] [ev:michael-hyatt-0392]

- **Tax and jurisdiction:** Canadian tax competitiveness affects his tax-adjusted return and willingness to deploy, but the evidence does not establish a categorical refusal to invest in Canada. [ev:michael-hyatt-0104] [ev:michael-hyatt-0105]

- **Source limitations:** Numerous pages repeat the same interviews or company announcements, so repetition is not independent corroboration. Several media files lacked transcripts, many pages lack publication dates, and aggregator entries conflict on counts, sectors, stages, and check sizes. Relative phrases such as the DataStealth article’s “earlier that month” are preserved without manufacturing a calendar closing date. Founder, executive, board, advisor, media, philanthropic, and corporate-financing relationships have therefore been kept separate from personal or family-office investments.
