# Michael Hyatt — Recurring Investment Theses

## Direct company investment

- **Explicit — back the entrepreneur before the initial plan.** Because companies pivot, Hyatt prioritizes character, trust, compatibility, adaptability, coachability, and the prospect of working together for roughly a decade. [ev:michael-hyatt-0016] [ev:michael-hyatt-0017] [ev:michael-hyatt-0185] [ev:michael-hyatt-0260] [ev:michael-hyatt-0264]

- **Explicit — pair excellent people with attractive markets.** Founder quality is his strongest recurring predictor, but he says a poor market can defeat an excellent team; substantial, growing, underserved markets are preferable. [ev:michael-hyatt-0261] [ev:michael-hyatt-0262] [ev:michael-hyatt-0334] [ev:michael-hyatt-0374]

- **Explicit — favor B2B economics.** He prefers B2B and positively evaluates scalable, defensible enterprise companies with high margins, recurring revenue, and a major customer problem; capital-intensive consumer scaling is less attractive. [ev:michael-hyatt-0009] [ev:michael-hyatt-0010] [ev:michael-hyatt-0177] [ev:michael-hyatt-0178]

- **Explicit — customer purchasing is the validation authority.** Actual budget allocation, referenceable paying customers, repeat purchases, and account expansion matter more than founder or investor enthusiasm. [ev:michael-hyatt-0005] [ev:michael-hyatt-0006] [ev:michael-hyatt-0121] [ev:michael-hyatt-0233] [ev:michael-hyatt-0234]

- **Explicit — differentiation must be consequential.** Hyatt favors fundamentally new capability and roughly order-of-magnitude cost or performance improvement; weak IP, incremental improvements, and superficial AI wrappers undermine the case. [ev:michael-hyatt-0036] [ev:michael-hyatt-0061] [ev:michael-hyatt-0062] [ev:michael-hyatt-0240] [ev:michael-hyatt-0244]

- **Explicit — economics before vanity growth.** He prioritizes early sales, gross margin, unit economics, a credible net-margin path, and controlled burn; he rejects negative-gross-margin growth and free audience land grabs as viable business models. [ev:michael-hyatt-0075] [ev:michael-hyatt-0220] [ev:michael-hyatt-0223] [ev:michael-hyatt-0225] [ev:michael-hyatt-0229]

- **Explicit — buy well.** Entry valuation, plausible ordinary exits, downside, and return relative to personal effort matter; even the right founder and company can be a poor investment at the wrong price. [ev:michael-hyatt-0067] [ev:michael-hyatt-0073] [ev:michael-hyatt-0226] [ev:michael-hyatt-0227] [ev:michael-hyatt-0271]

- **Explicit — active value-add must justify concentration.** He makes few direct investments, places larger amounts in a small number, and prefers opportunities where operating help, board work, or scaling advice can materially change the outcome. [ev:michael-hyatt-0020] [ev:michael-hyatt-0069] [ev:michael-hyatt-0070] [ev:michael-hyatt-0268] [ev:michael-hyatt-0378]

- **Inferred — founder-origin problems, focus, and steady execution are positive lenses.** His advice favors beginning with a real problem, concentrating on one initiative, hiring well, and accumulating operational wins; these are not all directly stated investment gates. [ev:michael-hyatt-0027] [ev:michael-hyatt-0029] [ev:michael-hyatt-0030] [ev:michael-hyatt-0150] [ev:michael-hyatt-0176]

## Sector and technology themes

- **Explicit — durable interest in cloud, security, and AI.** Hyatt has called these categories attractive and early, while expecting a small number of substantive AI companies to produce disproportionate outcomes. [ev:michael-hyatt-0041] [ev:michael-hyatt-0042] [ev:michael-hyatt-0057] [ev:michael-hyatt-0138]

- **Tension — AI optimism alongside valuation and utility concerns.** He remains bullish on AI’s broad impact but has warned of bubble pricing, gimmicky entrants, insufficient practical utility, crowded competition, and the need for customer value rather than AI as the pitch. [ev:michael-hyatt-0059] [ev:michael-hyatt-0060] [ev:michael-hyatt-0241] [ev:michael-hyatt-0246] [ev:michael-hyatt-0281] [ev:michael-hyatt-0282]

- **Explicit boundary — invest inside competence.** He largely avoids fashionable categories he does not understand and specifically names clean technology and health technology as exclusions from his own investing, notwithstanding thematic comments about telemedicine or AI healthcare. [ev:michael-hyatt-0279] [ev:michael-hyatt-0280] [ev:michael-hyatt-0396] [ev:michael-hyatt-0064]

- **Inferred — quantum is enabling but early.** He has described superconductors as foundational to quantum while separately characterizing quantum as significant but premature. [ev:michael-hyatt-0193] [ev:michael-hyatt-0198] [ev:michael-hyatt-0310]

## Timing and geography

- **Explicit/inferred — adversity can improve startup formation and selective deployment.** He has favored founding technology companies in downturns and viewed COVID dislocation as accelerating digital transformation and creating deployment opportunities, without endorsing routine market timing. [ev:michael-hyatt-0001] [ev:michael-hyatt-0139] [ev:michael-hyatt-0371] [ev:michael-hyatt-0372]

- **Explicit — hold for the long term.** Direct investments are underwritten with at least a ten-year intended holding period, while public equities follow buy-and-hold discipline rather than active trading. [ev:michael-hyatt-0276] [ev:michael-hyatt-0278]

- **Inferred — Canada can be the base, but the United States is an important commercial market.** He favors preserving a Canadian base while adopting an American-market orientation because of larger, faster, more risk-tolerant customers; Canadian tax competitiveness can affect willingness to deploy. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0032] [ev:michael-hyatt-0065] [ev:michael-hyatt-0066] [ev:michael-hyatt-0104] [ev:michael-hyatt-0105]

## Fund allocation and private equity

- **Explicit — private equity is preferred to venture as a standalone allocation.** Hyatt favors larger ownership and control, more repeatable three-to-four-times outcomes, and the ability to intervene when execution fails; he is skeptical of venture’s dependence on unpredictable outliers. [ev:michael-hyatt-0097] [ev:michael-hyatt-0098] [ev:michael-hyatt-0099] [ev:michael-hyatt-0101] [ev:michael-hyatt-0102] [ev:michael-hyatt-0103]

- **Explicit — illiquidity and operating burden require a premium.** Private equity should materially outperform public equities when ownership carries greater risk, work, and illiquidity. [ev:michael-hyatt-0331] [ev:michael-hyatt-0333]

- **Context-reported fund-selection practice.** CNBC reported that he favored funds whose founders committed substantial personal capital and whose access was constrained; he also benchmarks managers against peers and uses AI to analyze fund reports. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0105] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0192] [ev:michael-hyatt-0114] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0062]

## Public markets

- **Explicit — preservation-oriented buy and hold.** The family office avoids active trading, options, shorts, and AI-based stock selection; it seeks risk-adjusted returns rather than maximizing risk. [ev:michael-hyatt-0183] [ev:michael-hyatt-0184] [ev:michael-hyatt-0275] [ev:michael-hyatt-0276]

- **Context-reported implementation.** As of the 2022 reporting, the office largely used Vanguard ETFs plus selected large technology stocks and rarely changed the portfolio; separate direct speech identified purchases of Shopify and Fastly. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0189] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0190] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0007]

- **Exception — Bitcoin is tactical, not thesis-consistent.** Hyatt owns or owned Bitcoin exposure, including through a Fidelity ETF, but characterizes it as a disliked trade rather than a productive long-term asset. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0034] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0056] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0058] [ev:michael-hyatt-0398]

## Real estate and operating-company strategy

- **Context-reported real estate strategy.** The family office used funds investing in commercial property in secondary cities and also allocated materially to income-producing real-estate and debt structures. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0103] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0191] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0523]

- **Explicit — operating-company ownership is the high-upside barbell end.** Direct acquisitions or major stakes supply the highest potential return, while public equities provide more standard returns; his preferred direct model is concentrated and actively assisted. [ev:michael-hyatt-0094] [ev:michael-hyatt-0095] [ev:michael-hyatt-0070]

- **Different investment roles — angel investing, control and later-stage exposure.** Sources describe angel and startup co-investment alongside private equity, majority control, operating-company acquisitions, and Northleaf work with larger, later-stage, profitable companies. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0002] [ev:michael-hyatt-0096] [ev:michael-hyatt-0098] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0107]
