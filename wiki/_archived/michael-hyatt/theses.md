# Michael Hyatt — Recurring Investment Theses

- **Direct private-company investing—people first, explicitly:** He prioritizes founders and CEOs he likes, trusts, and can work with for about a decade; integrity, humility, kindness, listening, ambition, and pivot capacity recur as explicit criteria. [ev:michael-hyatt-0018] [ev:michael-hyatt-0034] [ev:michael-hyatt-0134] [ev:michael-hyatt-0310] [ev:michael-hyatt-0315]

- **Direct private-company investing—market pull, explicitly:** Customer budget, purchase, retention, and expansion are his strongest validation signals; an MVP without customers is not enough, while “land and expand” indicates that a company may truly exist. [ev:michael-hyatt-0008] [ev:michael-hyatt-0009] [ev:michael-hyatt-0217] [ev:michael-hyatt-0262]

- **Large markets and consequential problems, explicitly:** He prefers large markets with product-market fit and businesses solving major, necessity-driven problems; he has said this can outweigh relative team competence. [ev:michael-hyatt-0083] [ev:michael-hyatt-0085] [ev:michael-hyatt-0189]

- **B2B and enterprise economics, explicitly:** He likes B2B, enterprise sales, recurring revenue, and large margins; he views consumer models as harder because they often demand greater capital and velocity. [ev:michael-hyatt-0010] [ev:michael-hyatt-0011] [ev:michael-hyatt-0154] [ev:michael-hyatt-0188]

- **Defensibility and step-change products, explicitly:** Scalable, defensible businesses fit; in AI especially, he seeks non-obvious insight and roughly tenfold cost or performance gains rather than modest improvements or analogy-based positioning. [ev:michael-hyatt-0153] [ev:michael-hyatt-0062] [ev:michael-hyatt-0226] [ev:michael-hyatt-0231]

- **Focused execution, inferred from operating advice:** He favors doing one thing well, repeating a precise use case, and accumulating small operating gains rather than chasing one breakthrough. This is not documented as a universal investment gate. [ev:michael-hyatt-0028] [ev:michael-hyatt-0030] [ev:michael-hyatt-0031] [ev:michael-hyatt-0084]

- **Capital-efficient growth, explicitly and inferred:** Strong gross margins, sound unit economics, profitability, and revenue growth without margin erosion are preferred. His warnings about cash-flow dependence describe financing risk and survival, not an absolute refusal to fund every loss-making company. [ev:michael-hyatt-0075] [ev:michael-hyatt-0077] [ev:michael-hyatt-0253] [ev:michael-hyatt-0324]

- **Dilution discipline, inferred from operating history:** BlueCat’s bootstrapping and his criticism of capital raised without productive use support a preference for preserving founder ownership and avoiding dilution for its own sake. [ev:michael-hyatt-0002] [ev:michael-hyatt-0212] [ev:michael-hyatt-0213] He also recognizes that companies generally take more time and capital than planned, creating tension between avoiding wasteful dilution and maintaining adequate reserves. [ev:michael-hyatt-0014] [ev:michael-hyatt-0190]

- **Valuation discipline, explicitly:** A great founder and company can remain a poor investment at the wrong price. He avoids very early assets with valuations unsupported by progress and tests entry value against realistic exit distributions. [ev:michael-hyatt-0066] [ev:michael-hyatt-0067] [ev:michael-hyatt-0073] [ev:michael-hyatt-0255]

- **Governance and control, explicitly:** He personally prefers private equity and larger or majority stakes that permit corrective action, while disliking fragmented minority positions with no real say. [ev:michael-hyatt-0096] [ev:michael-hyatt-0097]

- **Selective active ownership, explicitly:** His edge is fewer, larger investments where he can apply operating experience; direct involvement can include boards, sales, go-to-market help, and bringing in complementary co-investors. [ev:michael-hyatt-0069] [ev:michael-hyatt-0070] [ev:michael-hyatt-0022] [ev:michael-hyatt-0023]

- **VC co-investment despite VC skepticism, explicitly:** He is skeptical of venture capital as a standalone asset class because outlier dependence and high failure rates make consistent outperformance difficult. [ev:michael-hyatt-0095] [ev:michael-hyatt-0098] [ev:michael-hyatt-0099] [ev:michael-hyatt-0106] Yet he likes co-investing beside capable VCs for diligence, governance, aligned terms, and long-term rights protection. [ev:michael-hyatt-0321] [ev:michael-hyatt-0322] [ev:michael-hyatt-0323]

- **Private equity over venture outlier hunting, explicitly:** He favors private equity’s more repeatable three-to-four-times-return model over dependence on rare 100-times or 1,000-times venture outcomes. [ev:michael-hyatt-0100] [ev:michael-hyatt-0101]

- **Fund allocation, source-reported:** The family office invests in VC and PE funds, funds of funds, and co-investments; reporting says Hyatt favors managers who commit substantial personal capital, while his own process benchmarks quarterly fund statements against market quartiles. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0018] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0094] [ev:michael-hyatt-0114]

- **Public markets, source-reported:** CNBC reported in 2022 that the family office rarely traded its equity portfolio and primarily used Vanguard ETFs plus selected Google, Microsoft, and Apple holdings. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0095] This slow approach matches his broader description of good investing as boring and consistent. [ev:michael-hyatt-0092] [ev:michael-hyatt-0093]

- **Real estate and income strategies, source-reported:** The family office has used real-estate funds, including one buying commercial properties in secondary cities, and Hyatt has described substantial investment in income-producing real-estate and debt structures. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0168] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0479]

- **Family-office preservation, explicitly:** After entrepreneurial wealth creation, the objective shifts toward preservation, disciplined rejection, and risk-adjusted return rather than boundary-pushing. [ev:michael-hyatt-0158] [ev:michael-hyatt-0159] [ev:michael-hyatt-0160] [ev:michael-hyatt-0304]

- **Portfolio allocation evolved or was described differently over time:** A 2020 account divided assets roughly among public markets, real estate, and private equity including venture; CNBC’s 2022 report similarly used thirds, while a later account described thirds in public stocks, predominantly PE with some VC, and acquisitions or major direct investments. [ctx:michael-hyatt-0477] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0092] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0050] These may reflect evolution, categorization differences, or imprecision rather than a true contradiction.

- **Technology-cycle thesis, explicit and inferred:** Hyatt has long viewed internet, cloud, security, AI, and eventually quantum as major secular shifts. [ev:michael-hyatt-0043] [ev:michael-hyatt-0139] [ev:michael-hyatt-0272] His AI view combines long-term conviction with near-term skepticism: AI is early and underrated, but many products are gimmicky and valuations exhibit bubble conditions. [ev:michael-hyatt-0058] [ev:michael-hyatt-0059] [ev:michael-hyatt-0105] [ev:michael-hyatt-0225]

- **Downturn formation and deployment, inferred from repeated operating and market comments:** He views recessions and crises as attractive times to start companies or selectively buy assets because competition and valuations decline, while requiring companies to revise plans and protect cash. [ev:michael-hyatt-0001] [ev:michael-hyatt-0053] [ev:michael-hyatt-0307] [ev:michael-hyatt-0319]

- **Canada-to-US scaling, inferred:** He views Canada as a strong company-building base and the United States as an especially attractive sales market and capital source because customers and investors are larger and more risk-tolerant. [ev:michael-hyatt-0118] [ev:michael-hyatt-0119] [ev:michael-hyatt-0064]

- **Operating-company strategy—build for optionality, inferred:** He advises building a great underlying business rather than celebrating fundraising or engineering exit desperation; profits and customer value preserve strategic choices. [ev:michael-hyatt-0007] [ev:michael-hyatt-0179] [ev:michael-hyatt-0301]

- **Operating-company strategy—DataStealth-specific:** The investment thesis is that breaches are eventually inevitable and AI increases sensitive data and network risk; DataStealth’s strong IP, simple deployment, profitability, margins, lack of debt, and limited prior outside capital supported an unusually concentrated, active majority investment. [ev:michael-hyatt-0071] [ev:michael-hyatt-0072] [ev:michael-hyatt-0167] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0212] [ev:michael-hyatt-0170]

- **Bitcoin/public speculative exposure evolved:** In a 2020 interview he said he was not bullish long term and regarded his holding as a trade, while a later discussion characterized limited exposure as a necessary hedge, recommended an ETF-and-hold route, and expressly capped his recommendation at 5% of liquid net worth. [ev:michael-hyatt-0328] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0054] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0055] [ev:michael-hyatt-0102]

- **Mission-oriented allocation is separate from return investing:** Hyatt calls his support for Creative Destruction Lab one of his best “investments” but explicitly clarifies that it was a donation, not a monetary-return investment. [ev:michael-hyatt-0104] [ctx:michael-hyatt-0057]
