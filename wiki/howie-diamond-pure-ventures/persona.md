# Howie Diamond — Investment Memory

This memory resolves to Howie Diamond, the early-stage venture investor associated most recently with Pure Ventures—not any same-name person. Pure Ventures’ own materials describe it as the third fund he co-founded and managed, after Ranch Ventures and Alpha Bridge Ventures, while multiple 2023–2025 publisher descriptions continue to identify him with Pure Ventures. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0032] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0050] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0001] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0078]

Scope and source limitations: the strongest decision-policy evidence consists of attributed first-person statements and two 2018 Alpha Bridge essays, one solely bylined to Diamond and one jointly bylined with Kari Sulenes. Jointly authored views should not be treated as exclusively his. Several current-looking firm, role, sector, and portfolio claims lack publication dates; they are valid only as of their undated source pages. Podcast audio without transcripts cannot supply additional investment rules. Repeated episode descriptions are not independent corroboration, and aggregator records are reported claims rather than independently verified holdings. The evidence is rich on founder assessment and support but sparse on market sizing, pricing, ownership targets, reserves, return thresholds, geography, governance rights, and formal deal construction.

## founder_team

Diamond’s central early-stage rule is to underwrite the people first. He says he started his fund because he believes he can identify talent and read people well, and that at the early stage he is “mostly betting on people.” [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0001] In his 2018 writing, he similarly says investors must consider “people variables” first and that businesses often create value in ways that manifest their founders’ characteristics. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0011] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0012] A jointly authored Alpha Bridge formulation makes the ordering explicit: qualitative factors before quantitative ones, or “people over businesses.” [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0006] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0007]

Selective In preference: founders with natural leadership ability who inspire others and can retain the talent needed to execute their ambitions. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0019] He has expressly assessed portfolio founders for grit, determination, perseverance, confidence, resilience, drive, relentlessness, willingness to believe in the impossible, and a highly competitive determination to win. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0020] His conviction also rests on whether a founder can “figure it out,” exercise sound judgment, and persist through adversity. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0013]

Character and relational fit matter alongside capability. Diamond says the parties’ moral and ethical codes need to align. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0002] His unusually personal “Sunday test” is explicit: he must be able to imagine spending informal Sunday time with every founder he backs, or he will not invest. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0003] Accordingly, a talented founder can still be Out when ethical alignment or durable interpersonal compatibility is absent; this is one of the few clearly stated rejection boundaries in the record. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0002] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0003]

Diamond treats leadership capacity as something that both constrains the company and can be developed. He wrote that companies grow only within the bounds of their leaders’ abilities and advanced an Alpha Bridge thesis that founders who build themselves create more sustainable and resilient companies. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0021] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0014] Inferred diligence questions, grounded in these views, include: Can this founder recruit and retain excellent people? Do they inspire trust? Will they keep learning as the organization changes? Can they make principled decisions under pressure and recover from setbacks? This translation from his founder-development advice into selection questions is tentative, not a disclosed scorecard. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0013] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0019] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0021]

Founder wellness is neither a cosmetic benefit nor permission to glorify exhaustion. Diamond believes healthy, happy founders are better positioned to overcome scaling challenges, and he rejects the idea of profiting from a company at the expense of its founder’s wellness and personal development. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0017] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0022] This creates a meaningful tension with his stated admiration for relentlessness and winning “at all costs”: the record supports intense ambition, but also an ethical boundary against returns built on founder self-destruction. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0020] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0022]

## market_opportunity

The record provides no explicit minimum market size, growth-rate threshold, geography, or required category structure. Market leadership today is not enough: in a jointly authored essay, Diamond and Sulenes argue that being first or being the present market leader does not predict where tomorrow’s value will be created. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0008] Their stated alternative is to look to the people behind the company when forecasting future value creation. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0009]

Source descriptions emphasize different sectors; they do not establish a mandate change. A November 2023 episode description emphasizes CPG investing, while 2025 descriptions frame his strategy as balancing consumer and frontier technology. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0003] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0004] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0010] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0012] An aggregator more specifically characterizes Pure Ventures as spanning CPG, e-commerce, and marketplaces on the consumer side and biotech, AI, robotics, and aerospace in deep tech, but this is source-reported firm positioning rather than Diamond’s own stated taxonomy. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0064]

Selective In preference therefore appears broad by sector but narrow by stage and founder fit: early-stage companies in consumer or frontier/deep-tech categories may fit, subject to people quality, execution, ethics, and the investor’s ability to add value. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0022] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0080] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0001] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0002] This is not evidence of equal appetite across every named vertical, nor of fixed sector allocations.

## competition_defensibility

Diamond does not equate first-mover status or current category leadership with durable advantage. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0008] His people-first framework instead implies that adaptability, leadership growth, talent retention, judgment, and persistence help determine whether an apparently advantaged company continues creating value. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0009] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0013] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0019]

Inferred—not an express moat requirement—he would likely probe whether the team can keep recruiting, learning, and executing after an initial product or timing advantage fades. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0008] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0019] The evidence does not establish preferences regarding patents, network effects, switching costs, proprietary data, regulatory barriers, brand defensibility, or technical benchmarks.

## product_solution

The clearest product-level preference is that an investable case cannot remain only an idea or speculation: Diamond says, “we invest in execution.” [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0004] That statement supports a preference for tangible evidence of building and delivery, but it does not disclose a universal revenue, launch, or product-market-fit prerequisite.

Inferred diligence would ask what the team has actually shipped, learned, or operationally accomplished, and how that evidence demonstrates its ability to adapt. This is an application of Diamond’s execution and “figure it out” language, not a disclosed product checklist. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0004] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0013] No evidence supports universal rules about product origin, founder-problem fit, technical architecture, IP ownership, gross margins, or customer concentration.

## traction_growth

Execution is a stronger validation signal than narrative alone. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0004] In the Alpha Bridge period, Diamond stated that the firm invested post-seed in companies with a clear path to Series A and spent the first eight to twelve months preparing founders for growth stages. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0015] This is a historically specific 2018 Alpha Bridge mandate, not necessarily Pure Ventures’ current stage rule.

The later Pure Ventures materials describe the firm more generally as early-stage and emphasize helping founders transition into leaders as their companies scale. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0048] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0049] Strong validation would therefore include demonstrated execution plus a credible organizational and financing path into the next growth stage, while the evidence does not establish mandatory revenue, retention, growth, customer, or fundraising metrics. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0004] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0015]

## business_model_economics

There is no direct evidence of Diamond’s required gross margin, burn multiple, CAC payback, unit economics, pricing power, capital efficiency, ownership, or target returns. The closest economic thesis is “human-powered ROI”: founder health, leadership capacity, and organizational resilience are treated as contributors to company performance and investor outcomes. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0014] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0017] Pure Ventures’ firm thesis likewise states that company health is directly proportional to founder health. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0031]

Alpha Bridge operationalized that belief by allocating part of its fund to leadership, wellness, and personal-support services delivered through Project Atlas. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0068] This indicates willingness to treat founder development as a portfolio resource rather than mere advice, but it does not quantify the allocation or prove a particular financial return from the program.

Inferred diligence may consider whether the company’s demands are sustainable for the founder and whether improved leadership can remove scaling constraints. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0021] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0022] Those considerations complement rather than replace conventional economics; the sources do not show that Diamond ignores quantitative analysis, only that the Alpha Bridge framework placed qualitative analysis first. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0007]

## deal_terms_valuation

The Pitch reports a typical on-show check of $25,000–$30,000 and reports that Diamond deployed $80,000 across three program investments. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0035] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0036] One disclosed transaction was a $30,000 SAFE in Teamable at a $15 million post-money cap. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0040] These are program-specific receipts and should not be generalized into Pure Ventures’ institutional check size, valuation ceiling, or preferred security.

A Waveup page displays several incompatible “average check” bands—$500,000–$1 million, $3–10 million, and $10–50 million—without resolving what they represent. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0067] They are too ambiguous to use as a mandate. There is no reliable evidence for target ownership, follow-on reserves, lead/follow posture, board requirements, pro rata policy, dilution limits, valuation discipline, or other standard terms.

## timing

Historically, Alpha Bridge described its entry point as the moment founder-CEOs were being called to transition into leaders of scaling companies. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0005] Diamond’s separately bylined 2018 account narrowed that historical strategy to post-seed companies with a clear Series A path. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0015] The associated support work occupied roughly the first eight to twelve months after investment. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0015]

Pure Ventures is now reported more broadly as an early-stage firm, while its support model still focuses on founders’ transition into leadership during scaling. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0022] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0049] The evidence therefore suggests continuity in the leadership-transition thesis but does not prove that Pure inherited Alpha Bridge’s exact post-seed/Series A entry constraint.

## investor_fit_constraints

The strongest fit is a founder who wants a close, human-centered investor relationship and welcomes leadership and wellness support rather than capital alone. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0016] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0018] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0023] Pure Ventures says it combines early-stage investment with founder development through Heartwood Collective, while source reporting links the firm to Pilea as a sister founder-health and leadership platform. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0048] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0049] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0065]

Selective In: high-integrity founders with leadership presence, recruiting and retention ability, grit, adaptable execution, and openness to developing themselves as the business scales. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0002] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0013] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0019] Selective Out: relationships that fail the ethical-alignment or Sunday-compatibility tests, and opportunities supported only by ideas or speculation rather than execution. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0002] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0003] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0004] Another explicit boundary is unwillingness to celebrate returns achieved by compromising founder wellness and development. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0022]

Tentative inferred diligence questions are: Who is this founder under stress? Can they inspire and retain a team? What have they executed rather than merely proposed? How do they respond when the original plan fails? Are their ethics compatible with a long relationship? Are they willing to grow personally as their role changes? Is the operating model compatible with sustainable founder health? [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0002] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0004] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0013] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0019] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0021] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0022] These questions synthesize explicit preferences and operating advice; except for the moral-alignment and Sunday tests, they should not be treated as mandatory gates.

## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

Diamond’s most distinctive feature is not a sector thesis but an attempt to redesign the investor-founder relationship around human development. Pure Ventures characterizes him as a progressive fund manager seeking new approaches to that relationship, and its firm model embeds founder development into early-stage investing. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0034] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0048] Alpha Bridge’s earlier version used Project Atlas and allocated fund resources to leadership, wellness, and personal support. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0068]

This orientation grew from personal operating experience: Diamond describes himself as a former entrepreneur, solo fund manager, and professional artist who experienced anxiety, doubt, sleeplessness, long workweeks, and the volatility of building. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0074] Source reporting also says burnout and imposter syndrome helped inspire Alpha Bridge and Atlas. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0017] His biography includes professional music, founding and selling Lo-Fi Music, and business-development work with startups at Sparkart, offering context for his emphasis on people and creative careers without itself proving an investment criterion. [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0002] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0038] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0039]

The unusual policy is thus dual: back unusually driven leaders while refusing to treat personal collapse as an acceptable cost of ambition. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0020] [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0022] His record treats founder wellness as an investment and governance concern, investor support as an active responsibility, and leadership development as part of portfolio construction rather than an optional perk. [ev:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0018] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0049] [ctx:howie-diamond-pure-ventures-0068]
