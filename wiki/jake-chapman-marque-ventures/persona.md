# Jake Chapman — Investment Memory

This memory covers the resolved Jake Chapman associated with Marque Ventures, not same-name individuals. It synthesizes verified investor speech and clearly attributed quotations with source-reported context through March 4, 2026. Chapman is reported as Marque Ventures’ founder/Managing Director or partner; the firm targets early-stage technologies aligned with U.S. national-security and allied needs. His earlier roles include managing AVCC and co-founding Alpha Bridge Ventures and Gelt VC. Titles vary by source and date. ## founder_team

Chapman’s strongest explicit founder signal is authentic commitment. A founder should visibly care about the company or problem, because the journey supplies repeated reasons to quit; low energy during a meeting makes it difficult for him to become enthusiastic. He especially associates downturn founders with persistence and expresses a strong preference for working with veterans. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0009] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0010] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0016] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0048]

Thoughtfulness matters more than instant perfection. His team is especially impressed by considered answers to hard questions, while an honest “I need to think” followed by a strong written response is a positive signal. References are a routine diligence tool, and his broader writing treats trustworthiness and reputation as essential in decade-long venture relationships. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0011] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0012] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0059] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0076]

In national security, his operating advice strongly favors a co-founder or very early employee with firsthand military service or defense-acquisition experience. This is advice rather than a universal disclosed investment prerequisite, but it indicates what he sees as a major execution advantage. He explicitly likes veteran entrepreneurs and attributes persistence, mission orientation, and cross-role adaptability to veteran talent. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0005] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0016] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0101]

Inferred: he will probe whether the team combines relevant experience, a record of execution, and the ability to evolve. He warns about all-novice management and values teams that have built together, but those comments were framed as employment advice, not absolute investment gates. He also distinguishes zero-to-one creation from scaling and generally opposes forcibly replacing founder-CEOs except in extreme circumstances; stalls, lost passion, inability to inspire, and failure to recruit or generate next steps are warning signs. ## market_opportunity

Chapman needs a concrete customer and purchase rationale before accepting a market-size slide. He asks whether the target market is genuinely open, how large it is, whether the problem is painful enough to change behavior, and whether actual end-user demand exists. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0051] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0052] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0057] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0106] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0112]

His central current opportunity is a structural transfer of defense spending toward startups. He argues that U.S. capability needs and pressure on incumbent suppliers leave the government little choice but to redirect budgets, while crises expand budgets and political support for diversifying the industrial base. The firm reports targeting American defense, manufacturing, and deep technology aligned with Department of Defense and Intelligence Community needs. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0019] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0020] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0094]

He sees defense as counter-cyclical and venture as low-correlated with other assets, making each potentially diversifying at the total-portfolio level. These are allocation arguments, not claims that an individual defense startup is low risk. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0014] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0018]

His sector map is broad: aerospace, defense, drones, space, flight, contested communications, positioning/navigation/timing in GPS-denied environments, advanced manufacturing, directed energy, contested logistics, energy, AI, quantum computing, synthetic biology, climate technology, and ordinary enabling products with defense applications. He sees present terrestrial value from space infrastructure and a later native in-space economy. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0015] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0025] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0079] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0080] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0119]

## competition_defensibility

Chapman treats “we have no competitors” and casual dismissal of known rivals as credibility failures. He expects founders to know the landscape and explain differentiation through customer value rather than feature lists. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0013] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0105]

A diligence question he explicitly asks is whether technology, market structure, or data creates durable advantage. Inferred: this is a recurring defensibility test, not proof that every investment must possess all three mechanisms. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0114]

Defense specialization itself can supply an investing edge: security clearances institutionalize information asymmetry, and procurement dysfunction creates potential alpha for people who know how to navigate it. He also acknowledges that this edge shrinks as the system improves. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0116] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0117]

He considers small unmanned aerial systems crowded and instead points to less-saturated areas such as contested communications and logistics, denied-environment navigation, directed energy, and advanced manufacturing. This is a sector view at the time of the source, not a permanent exclusion of every sUAS company. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0025]

## product_solution

A strong product story communicates the unique value proposition immediately, explains what the product does, and makes its intended differentiation legible within the opening slides. Novel technology or an original application of technology is generally important to fit. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0103] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0104] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0121]

Inferred: Chapman favors products built from early contact with identified end users. He advises founders in every sector to know the end user and obtain early feedback; in food and beverage he calls real customer feedback essential for product refinement and buyer or distributor validation. These are strong validation preferences, not stated prerequisites for every investment. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0006] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0065]

He is willing to change a skeptical product view when data contradict intuition. Conversely, a technically functional product may remain unattractive if it does not solve a meaningful problem, induce adoption, or deliver a materially better experience. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0046] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0051] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0111] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0113]

## traction_growth

Completed execution carries more weight than plans. He asks what has already been done, wants granular roadmaps with dates rather than aspirations, and views communicated milestones that are subsequently exceeded as a way to build investor trust. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0039] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0053] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0054]

For defense, grants, a single contract, and verbal interest are not “real success.” Meaningful de-risking includes user demand, contracting authority, an available budget, a path into requirements and programs of record, and evidence of actual adoption. Pilot-to-commercial conversion is also critical evidence in ordinary enterprise diligence. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0023] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0029] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0108] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0187]

Revenue forecasts should combine credible near-term assumptions with venture-scale ambition: too much projected revenue appears delusional, while too little suggests a constrained opportunity or vision. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0110]

## business_model_economics

He accepts that an early business model will evolve, but dislikes a presentation that resembles indiscriminate experimentation. His shorthand for fit combines passion with profitability or a solid business, while his broader desired outcome combines positive impact with large financial value creation. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0086] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0096] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0107]

For hardware, he asks for bill-of-materials cost, hardware margin, manufacturing scalability, and capital expenditure under strong demand—especially when the upfront unit accounts for most lifetime value. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0055] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0058]

His preferred future defense-prime model is nimble and largely avoids cost-plus contracting, seeking more capability at lower customer cost while retaining higher supplier margins. He also regards Mettle Ops’ reported technology-transfer model favorably, although the evidence does not establish it as a holding. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0030] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0077]

Non-dilutive funding is useful when it finances the existing roadmap and the team has spare capacity; SBIR work becomes dangerous when it redirects the business. This is a financing-risk warning, not a refusal to fund grant-backed companies. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0027] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0028]

## deal_terms_valuation

Price can transform attractiveness: Chapman’s maxim is that every company is investable at the right price. He warns that fashionable defense deals may struggle to grow into elevated valuations, particularly if sector enthusiasm fades. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0022] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0087]

He has described a small-fund model in which roughly 1% ownership at a billion-dollar-plus exit could return the fund multiple times, while either several moderate exits or one unicorn could support a 3x net LP outcome. Those statements described Alpha Bridge’s fund construction, not necessarily Marque’s current targets. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0098] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0099]

He sees signal-following as potentially workable at pre-seed or seed if the fund moves fast and accepts a higher price. By Series A, competing capital, pro-rata rights, and ownership targets can make allocation prohibitively expensive. Milestone-based tranches can also make waiting for later de-risking more attractive than investing immediately. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0041] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0042] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0056]

The current firm says it can lead or follow Seed through Series A. A third-party profile reports a $100,000–$5 million range and $1.5 million sweet spot, but the source supplies no reliable as-of date; treat those figures as indicative rather than confirmed current mandate. [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0140] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0197]

## timing

Chapman describes venture as predicting the future and investing behind that vision—“skating” toward coming needs rather than funding only today’s battlefield. In defense, he looks to congressional priorities, acquisition signals, and multiyear spending plans to ask what must be funded now for demand expected over the next five years. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0001] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0002] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0003]

He argues that building for commercial industry first can delay defense-market fit by five to ten years and explicitly rejects a dual-use-only mandate: some important technologies should be built defense-first. This coexists with Marque being described in some sources as a defense-and-dual-use investor; the consistent boundary is a credible defense application, not compulsory commercial dual use. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0017] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0026] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0139] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0170]

His 2020 commentary was highly cycle-sensitive: he expected lower entry prices, stronger eventual exits, durable pandemic-driven behavioral changes, temporarily lower customer-acquisition costs, and near-term fundraising disruption. Those were historically situated views, not current forecasts. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0034] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0036] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0047] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0050]

## investor_fit_constraints

The clearest present mandate is early-stage emerging capability for the United States and allies, with a clear defense application. Sources describe Marque as investing in U.S., NATO, and allied national security, while Chapman expressly excludes purely consumer-facing companies, e-commerce, game studios, and adtech in one profile. [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0139] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0140] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0141] [ctx:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0166] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0120]

Mission and financial return are jointly important. Chapman says he shifted away from “interesting frivolities” toward technologies capable of materially advancing human prosperity, treats each investment as a vote for a desired future, and becomes especially excited when meaningful impact and large profit coincide. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0081] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0082] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0084] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0088] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0089] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0090]

Investor-founder fit is selective and reciprocal. Chapman believes GPs should invest in companies they are personally passionate about and defer when another GP is more enamored or better suited. He also favors advisors and investors who contribute relevant skills and networks rather than prestige alone. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0061] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0062] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0094] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0095]

A tentative inferred diligence sequence is: establish mission and mandate fit; identify the end user, buyer, budget, and purchase rationale; test founder commitment and candor; examine competition and durable advantage; distinguish grants or pilots from repeatable adoption; inspect unit economics, capital needs, and roadmap; and finally decide whether price and ownership can produce fund-level returns. This synthesis is not a disclosed scorecard. ## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

Chapman says many VC rejections reflect fund pacing, internal politics, portfolio conflicts, or sector preference rather than company quality. His own funnel—roughly 1,000–1,500 reviewed opportunities for about four investments in the cited discussion—encourages rapid disqualification, so a pass should not automatically be interpreted as a negative judgment on the business. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0007] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0008]

He does not ask who else is in a round until late and dismisses “proprietary deal flow” as a meaningful differentiator when it produces the same portfolios as everyone else. This suggests greater emphasis on independent judgment and specialist access than social proof, without proving that syndicate composition never matters. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0091] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0092]

Physical interaction and travel historically mattered to his assessment and sourcing because video can obscure interpersonal cues and trust signals, while visiting a city surfaces founders and ecosystems he might otherwise miss. These observations predate later remote-work normalization and should not be converted into an in-person-meeting requirement. [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0043] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0044] [ev:jake-chapman-marque-ventures-0045]

Finally, his impact conception has included founder health, employee culture and wellbeing, diverse founders, and mission-oriented companies; Alpha Bridge reported investing in founder development as part of its model. These historical fund practices illuminate his values but should not be assumed to be identical to Marque’s current defense mandate.
