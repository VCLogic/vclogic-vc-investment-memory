# Jenny Fielding — Investment Memory

This memory concerns the resolved Jenny Fielding associated with Everywhere Ventures; it excludes same-name people. It separates Fielding’s own attributed speech (`ev`) from source-reported context (`ctx`). The strongest first-person material comes from her own profiles and a direct Q&A; several 2026 market comments are textually attributed by PodLume but were not acoustically verified, so they should carry less weight. Firm policy and firm-reported portfolio relationships are not automatically personal policy, personal investments, deal leadership, or current ownership, and historical Techstars activity is distinct from Everywhere Ventures. Portfolio totals vary with date and scope—from more than 250 first investments to more than 300 investments—so they are directional rather than a reconciled ledger. A podcast appearance alone establishes an interview rather than an investment, but the primary Everywhere portfolio page separately establishes firm-reported portfolio relationships for companies it lists. ## founder_team

- **In — candor before polish.** Fielding says the founders she trusts most begin with the problem rather than the pitch. A 20-minute highlight reel that postpones the issue keeping the founder awake is a warning sign about communication, not an automatic rejection rule. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0011] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0012]

- **In — execution over credential proxies.** She criticizes venture’s reliance on Stanford, YC, Google, or similar surface credentials and points to founders in Lagos, Bogotá, and Tallinn who out-executed conventionally credentialed peers. The practical policy is to investigate what the team has actually accomplished under constraint instead of treating pedigree as quality. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0014] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0015]

- **In — globally minded, resilient builders.** Her “Everywhere Mindset” associates successful global entrepreneurs with resilience, conviction, and cultural curiosity. This is an expressed observation about founders, not a claim that all three traits are mandatory gates. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0021]

- **In — first-time and pre-traction founders remain eligible.** Everywhere’s stated mandate welcomes first-time founders and founders who lack traction but possess a distinct vision of a not-yet-existing world. That firm-level policy prevents overreading her later Series A traction warning as a pre-seed prerequisite. [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0230]

- **Inferred — preserve decisive agency.** From her operating philosophy, a founder who can absorb feedback without becoming immobilized is likely attractive; she says the early-stage danger is not merely a bad quarter but a founder second-guessed into paralysis. This is support advice applied tentatively to selection, not an expressly stated investment gate. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0013]

- **Out / caution — promotional opacity and credential-only cases.** Teams that conceal the central risk behind a polished narrative, or whose case rests mainly on institutional badges, conflict with her stated signals. These are concerns to diligence, not universal vetoes. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0012] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0014]

- **Questions explicitly suggested by her comments:** What is the real problem? What currently keeps the founder awake? What execution evidence exists independent of credentials? How has the team demonstrated resilience or cultural curiosity across its market? ## competition_defensibility

- **AI cost declines cut both ways.** Fielding observes that as model costs fall, barriers to entry can fall too. Cheap infrastructure may improve company economics while simultaneously making imitation easier; lower cost alone is therefore weak evidence of defensibility. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0004]

- **Moats must be reassessed, not presumed permanent.** A 2026 firm summary says volatility was causing investors to revisit assumptions, moats, and emotional resilience, while another host framed her discussion around competitive advantages disappearing faster than before. These are source summaries, but they reinforce a dynamic rather than static view of defensibility. [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0038] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0199]

- **In — advantage that compounds after commoditization. Out / caution — wrappers whose differentiation depends chiefly on expensive or scarce base models.** This is an inference from her barrier-to-entry observation, not an explicit rejection rule. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0004]

- **Questions supported by the record:** If the underlying model becomes cheaper and universally available, what remains difficult to reproduce? Which current assumptions about the moat could change? What evidence would cause the team and investor to revise the competitive story? [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0004] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0017] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0038]

## product_solution

- **Problem-first framing matters more than theatrical pitching.** Her strongest product-related decision signal is that trustworthy founders lead with the customer problem, while delayed disclosure of the hard issue is a flag. The product case should therefore make the pain, affected user, and unresolved risk legible early. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0011] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0012]

- **Inferred — founder-origin insight is useful but not required.** Her founder experience makes her attentive to the difficulty of building products, inspiring teams, and staying solvent; however, she does not state that founders must have personally experienced the problem. Do not manufacture such a gate. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0020]

- **Zero-to-one fit.** Fielding reports having worked with hundreds of founders at the zero-to-one phase, and Everywhere positions itself at the earliest stages. Product incompleteness is compatible with the mandate so long as the underlying vision and problem are distinctive. [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0122] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0198] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0230]

- **Questions:** What exact problem is being solved now? What is the least polished but most consequential product risk? What has been learned at zero-to-one, and what resource would most improve the company’s chance to survive and thrive? [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0011] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0012] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0020]

## traction_growth

- **Pre-seed traction is optional, not universally required.** Everywhere explicitly welcomes founders who may lack traction, which is consistent with investing before product-market fit. Distinctive vision can substitute for mature operating evidence at this stage. [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0230] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0116]

- **Later financing has a different bar.** Fielding described a backlog of seed companies with “nice traction” but not the roughly $3 million ARR and 30% month-over-month growth then expected for Series A progression. This records a market observation, not her pre-seed investment minimum and not a timeless Series A rule. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0007]

- **Strong validation versus requirement.** Early revenue, retention, or efficient growth would strengthen a case, but the evidence does not support converting any metric into a mandatory Everywhere gate. The quoted figures diagnose a financing chasm for existing seed companies. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0007] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0230]

- **Questions:** What evidence of demand exists today, even if it is not revenue? What milestones will make the next round financeable under current conditions? If growth is below later-stage market expectations, can the company reach stronger proof without exhausting cash? [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0007] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0006]

## investor_fit_constraints

- **Stage and geography.** Best fit is global pre-seed technology: Everywhere was founded in 2018, is headquartered in New York, invests worldwide, and describes itself as supporting founders at the earliest stage. [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0025] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0198] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0231]

- **Network model.** The firm reports hundreds of founder and operator LPs as both backing and deal-flow infrastructure; another source reports 30 geographically distributed venture partners supporting sourcing and diligence. Community participation is therefore central to the operating model. [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0025] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0006]

- **Founder-support fit.** Fielding’s claimed value is operator-informed empathy and access to resources that help founders survive and thrive. Founders seeking only passive capital may receive less of what differentiates her, although the evidence does not say passive-capital seekers are rejected. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0019] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0020]

- **Decision discipline after investment.** She warns that conviction and correctness differ and says the crucial judgment often comes when the story changes. Fit includes willingness to revisit assumptions honestly rather than defend the original narrative. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0016] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0017]

- **Boundary against stale pattern matching.** She views copying patterns from prior wins as a greater danger than excessive selectivity. Novel companies should not be penalized merely because they lack a familiar archetype. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0018]

- **Tentative inferred diligence checklist:** establish problem candor, execution independent of pedigree, distinctive vision, financing runway, a moat that survives cheaper inputs, and a valuation compatible with venture returns. This synthesis is inferred from separate comments and must not be treated as a published scorecard. ## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

- Fielding’s investment style is explicitly rooted in the humbling experience of founding companies, and she says an investor standard becomes theoretical when detached from the experience of making payroll. This operator empathy is both a sourcing lens and a post-investment behavior, not simply a sector thesis. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0008] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0019]

- Her self-correction rule is unusually explicit: strong conviction is not the same as correctness, entry judgment is less important than judgment when facts change, and successful patterns can become blinders. The distinctive policy is disciplined curiosity—form a view, then remain ready to revise it. [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0016] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0017] [ev:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0018]

- Everywhere treats community as investment infrastructure: founder and operator LPs contribute backing and deal flow, geographically distributed venture partners aid sourcing and diligence, and founder networks supply real-time market insight. This is a firm design choice, not evidence that any individual community member owns or approved every investment. [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0025] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0006] [ctx:jenny-fielding-everywhere-ventures-0200]

- Outside conventional venture taxonomy, source summaries associate her with conservative personal finance, early Bitcoin investments, teaching at Columbia and Cornell Tech, authorship of *Venture Everywhere*, and a compensated non-executive directorship at Decidr AI Industries/Live Verdure.
