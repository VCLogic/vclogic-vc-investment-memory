# Mac Conwell — Recurring Investment Theses

## Direct private-company investing

- **Explicit — overlooked geography and networks:** RareBreed primarily seeks exceptional pre-seed and seed companies outside California, Massachusetts, New York, and other capital-saturated hubs, while preserving discretion for exceptional hub-based or otherwise off-thesis deals. [ev:mac-conwell-0097] [ev:mac-conwell-0675] [ev:mac-conwell-0676]

- **Explicit — under-networked, not demographically exclusive:** Conwell frames the core thesis around founders denied conventional networks and capital access; Black and brown founders are an important underfunded opportunity, but founder race is not an eligibility restriction. [ev:mac-conwell-0043] [ev:mac-conwell-0098] [ev:mac-conwell-0099] [ev:mac-conwell-0386]

- **Explicit — venture scale governs:** A company must have a credible route to a billion-dollar-scale outcome and potentially return all or much of the fund; good, profitable businesses below venture scale may require other financing. [ev:mac-conwell-0213] [ev:mac-conwell-0214] [ev:mac-conwell-0420] [ev:mac-conwell-0425]

- **Explicit — customer acquisition over product worship:** The recurring operating lens is acquisition, customer experience, and retention. Distinctive or repeatable distribution, not technical superiority alone, is central to software underwriting. [ev:mac-conwell-0134] [ev:mac-conwell-0300] [ev:mac-conwell-0302]

- **Explicit — legacy consumer categories:** He favors physical consumer products entering categories with about a decade of limited innovation, despite inventory, overhead, and shipping risk; beauty and hair tools are a recurring example, while similar oils and creams are disfavored. [ev:mac-conwell-0159] [ev:mac-conwell-0160] [ev:mac-conwell-0301] [ev:mac-conwell-0341] [ev:mac-conwell-0342]

- **Explicit — founder exceptionalism has multiple proofs:** Ingenuity, execution, traction, deep problem commitment, grit, domain expertise, and unusual distribution can each establish conviction; no single founder archetype applies. [ev:mac-conwell-0277] [ev:mac-conwell-0304] [ev:mac-conwell-0835]

- **Explicit — strong business evidence outranks presentation:** Revenue, growth, monetization, retained customers, and verified metrics can overcome imperfect terminology or delivery; storytelling unsupported by numbers is a warning. [ev:mac-conwell-0458] [ev:mac-conwell-0460] [ev:mac-conwell-0509] [ev:mac-conwell-0561]

- **Inferred — authentic problem origin:** His praise for firsthand problem exposure suggests a preference for authentic founder-market fit, but it is not a universal prerequisite because he recognizes several other forms of founder exceptionalism. [ev:mac-conwell-0189] [ev:mac-conwell-0190] [ev:mac-conwell-0277]

- **Inferred — resource constraint as a revealing test:** His operating advice repeatedly values founders who acquire customers and build prototypes through creative, low-downside action before capital arrives; this is a favorable signal, not a mandatory hardship test. [ev:mac-conwell-0229] [ev:mac-conwell-0232] [ev:mac-conwell-0577]

## Evolution and tensions

- **Stage versus evidence:** RareBreed is an earliest-stage, often first-check investor, yet Conwell says most investments already have revenue and growth. Pre-product investments remain exceptional rather than forbidden, especially for extraordinary founders, technology, acquisition evidence, or long-stagnant physical-product markets. When rival opportunities already have a product, team, and revenue, he treats that contemporaneous progress as the competitive bar—not as a universal eligibility gate. [ev:mac-conwell-0690] [ev:mac-conwell-0403] [ev:mac-conwell-0243] [ev:mac-conwell-0618] [ev:mac-conwell-0239]

- **Thresholds versus comparison:** He has cited rough revenue and growth benchmarks, but also says RareBreed has no fixed revenue threshold and compares each company against the contemporaneous pipeline. The numbers therefore describe validation or market expectations, not mechanical investment gates. [ev:mac-conwell-0573] [ev:mac-conwell-0604] [ev:mac-conwell-0202] [ev:mac-conwell-0239]

- **Geographic focus versus global ambition:** The initial mandate emphasized overlooked U.S. regions and international investing was originally deferred; later statements permitted selective international deals, reported a Uganda investment, and expressed interest in Kenyan agriculture. [ev:mac-conwell-0359] [ev:mac-conwell-0704] [ev:mac-conwell-0705] [ev:mac-conwell-0770] [ev:mac-conwell-0771]

- **Concentration versus flexibility:** Early descriptions ranged from roughly 24–30 planned companies to later plans for 40–45, while a 2025 testimony reported 47 investments. The stable principle is that checks and early-stage multiples should allow a winner to return the fund, not a fixed portfolio count. [ctx:mac-conwell-1080] [ctx:mac-conwell-0512] [ctx:mac-conwell-0947] [ev:mac-conwell-0674]

- **Sector agnosticism versus exclusions:** The fund is broadly sector agnostic but explicitly excludes life sciences and has category-level dislikes, including crowded social networks and many dating or beauty-consumable products. Exceptional off-thesis opportunities remain possible, so dislikes should not be recast as permanent bans. [ev:mac-conwell-0083] [ev:mac-conwell-0316] [ev:mac-conwell-0339] [ev:mac-conwell-0340] [ev:mac-conwell-0753]

- **Diversity mission versus return discipline:** He advocates temporarily overweighting underrepresented founders and correcting access gaps, while repeatedly insisting that LP returns, not impact or identity alone, govern investment selection. [ev:mac-conwell-0294] [ev:mac-conwell-0212] [ev:mac-conwell-0416] [ev:mac-conwell-0764]

## Fund allocation and emerging-manager thesis

- **Explicit — venture-fund diversification:** For an outside allocator, Conwell prefers diversified exposure across multiple venture funds over concentrating the same capital in one startup or one fund, citing lower single-company loss concentration. [ev:mac-conwell-0092] [ev:mac-conwell-0093] [ev:mac-conwell-0477] [ev:mac-conwell-0478]

- **Explicit — access through smaller LP checks:** Investor-count limits and high minimums restrict participation; lower minimums can admit smaller LPs and let them diversify across managers. [ev:mac-conwell-0371] [ev:mac-conwell-0372] [ev:mac-conwell-0373] [ev:mac-conwell-0808]

- **Explicit — emerging-manager diligence should be contextual:** A first-time manager’s lack of personal wealth or conventional fund track record should not dominate diligence; demonstrated founder support and practical startup-assistance capability deserve greater weight. [ev:mac-conwell-0820] [ev:mac-conwell-0821] [ev:mac-conwell-0822] [ev:mac-conwell-0823]

- **Explicit — traditional fund over rolling fund:** He chose a traditional structure so every LP could share exposure to investments across the fund’s life and rejected quarterly participation economics that could exclude early LPs from later winners. [ev:mac-conwell-0510] [ev:mac-conwell-0679] [ev:mac-conwell-0680]

- **Firm evolution:** Conwell described a long-term ambition to combine direct company investing with fund-to-fund allocations as RareBreed becomes a Baltimore-based multistage firm; this was an aspiration, not evidence that such allocations had already begun. [ctx:mac-conwell-0105] [ctx:mac-conwell-0106]

## Public markets

- **Boundary:** His described core investment activity concerns privately held, non-exchange-listed companies; public-company commentary should not be mistaken for a RareBreed public-equities mandate. [ev:mac-conwell-0730]

- **Explicit observations, not a portfolio thesis:** He favored Oatly and Twitter in particular public-market discussions, but also warned that headline revenue and public listing status do not eliminate loss, valuation, or operating risk. [ev:mac-conwell-0528] [ev:mac-conwell-0536] [ev:mac-conwell-0537] [ev:mac-conwell-0539]

- **IPO outcome discipline:** An IPO is one possible venture exit, but he views the process as expensive, uncertain, and operationally demanding; startup-founder skills do not automatically transfer to public-company leadership. [ev:mac-conwell-0213] [ev:mac-conwell-0472] [ev:mac-conwell-0474] [ev:mac-conwell-0475]

## Real estate

No supplied evidence establishes a personal, RareBreed, or fund-allocation real-estate investment thesis. Real-estate or home-renovation companies mentioned in the portfolio record should not be converted into a view on owning property as an asset class.

## Operating-company strategy

- **Inferred — financing accelerates rather than creates the business:** His operating advice favors resolving the core problem, acquiring customers, and defining use of funds before fundraising; capital is an accelerant, not evidence of success. [ev:mac-conwell-0205] [ev:mac-conwell-0564] [ev:mac-conwell-0702]

- **Inferred — acquisition deserves product-level creativity:** Persistent outreach, unconventional channels, community distribution, and measurable paid acquisition are all viable when they produce repeatable customers economically. [ev:mac-conwell-0130] [ev:mac-conwell-0227] [ev:mac-conwell-0230] [ev:mac-conwell-0776]

- **Inferred — operational resilience:** Team accountability, milestone follow-through, cash-management preparedness, and multiple banking relationships reduce execution risk, but these observations are not stated as universal pre-investment gates. [ev:mac-conwell-0126] [ev:mac-conwell-0308] [ev:mac-conwell-0623] [ev:mac-conwell-0624]
