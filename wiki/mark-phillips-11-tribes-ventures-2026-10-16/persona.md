# Mark Phillips — Investment Memory

This memory covers the resolved Mark Phillips, founder and managing partner of Chicago-based 11 Tribes Ventures, and excludes same-name individuals. It synthesizes his attributed statements with source-reported context; repeated pages and duplicate transcripts are not independent corroboration. Many transcripts lack publication dates, several media files lack transcripts, and current-sounding claims should be read as of their source dates. Phillips also cautions that investors work from imperfect patterns, biases, and instinct rather than certainty. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0001] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0083]

His investment philosophy grew directly from Diasense, the diabetes hardware-and-software startup he built and later shut down after being overtaken by another product, losing expected financing, and running out of cash. That history informs—but does not by itself prove—his preference for capital efficiency, founder resilience, and direct confrontation of company risk. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0003] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0006] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0007] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0087]

## founder_team

- **People are the center of the underwriting.** Phillips says 11 Tribes focuses on founders, calls investment in people its return strategy, and would choose an A-team with a B-idea over an A-idea with a B-team. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0003] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0212] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0274]

- **In: a distinctive founder–market fit.** He wants founders with a “unique right to win,” often operators whose long industry experience exposed a painful problem they can solve efficiently. First-time founders are acceptable; several Fund II founders reportedly came directly from industry. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0051] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0058] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0059]

- **In: integrity, accountability, humility, drive, judgment, and service-oriented leadership.** His shorthand is “humble, hungry, and smart”; integrity and accountability sit near the top of his stated criteria, and servant leadership is an explicit preference. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0019] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0025] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0026]

- **Motivation is a core diligence question.** He asks what truly drives the founder, whether identity is bound to financial success, and whether the founder is solving a meaningful problem or mainly pursuing money. He regards primarily financial motivation as a durability risk when conditions deteriorate. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0022] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0033] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0034]

- **In: coachability and constructive conflict.** He values founders who admit they need help, accept feedback, and can engage in healthy conflict; an NQS team’s active request for more critical feedback was a favorable diligence signal. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0017] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0177] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0178]

- **In: team composition, not one ideal personality.** He says there is no single correct founder profile but there is a right way to compose a complementary team. The firm supplements instinct with psychometric profiling and, in a later first-party account, says Culture Index is used throughout investment diligence because “the founder is the asset.” [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0085] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0176] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0311]

- **Out: dishonesty and unresolved toxic relationships.** Overstating $600,000 of recurring revenue as roughly $1 million is, in his example, a consequential trust failure; unresolved interpersonal toxicity and co-founder conflict threaten execution. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0084] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0086] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0120]

- **Founder health is part of the investment system, not merely a benefit.** The firm allocates 2% of invested capital non-dilutively to coaching, counseling, leadership development, organizational health, and related resilience support. Phillips describes this as both care and a risk-mitigation/value-creation strategy. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0158] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0202] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0289]

- **Inferred, not a universal screening gate:** his operating advice favors multidimensional, self-aware leaders who rest, care for family, separate identity from company performance, manage ego, and remain accountable. These views illuminate fit but should not be converted into mandatory prerequisites unless explicitly stated. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0009] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0092] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0275] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0284]

## market_opportunity

- **In: expanding industries, but not necessarily giant present-day TAMs.** Phillips prefers strong founders in expanding industries, yet says precise present TAM matters less where a company can acquire customers and define a new category. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0101] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0112] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0113]

- **Small markets can be attractive.** A bounded niche may deter large competitors and still support the fund’s return model; his NQS example combined an approximately $250 million TAM, a weak incumbent, acute pain, and a forecast that the company could capture 50–60% of the market in 18–24 months. These figures were Phillips’s underwriting views, not independently verified outcomes. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0070] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0071] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0185]

- **In: niche blue-collar and vertical markets.** Waste management, wire harnessing, trauma registries, and other overlooked operating niches fit his model when domain depth and customer value are clear. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0082] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0220] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0252]

- **Market diligence emphasizes direction and urgency.** He asks where technology and customer behavior are moving rather than claiming to calculate TAM perfectly. Customer desperation for an imperfect product is stronger validation than a polished product with no revenue. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0114] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0187]

## competition_defensibility

- **Product code alone is increasingly a weak moat.** Phillips believes falling development barriers let competitors reproduce software rapidly; he makes the same critique of generic AI wrappers and foundational language-model businesses. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0139] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0131]

- **In: distribution and workflow integration.** He asks whether the company knows exactly whom it sells to, has working customer relationships, and integrates deeply enough into specific workflows that the AI-enabled solution is effective and used. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0245]

- **In: lower-cost access and community.** A unique niche or channel partnership that acquires customers faster and more cheaply is attractive; a customer community with shared identity may also be defensible. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0068] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0069]

- **In: learning systems.** In a later AI-era formulation, defensibility comes from systems that generate, process, and learn from data better than competitors, rather than from static possession of data or knowledge. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0276]

- **Competitive tension is deliberately sized.** Very large markets may invite frontier-lab competition; narrower domains can be protected by expertise and unattractive scale for those labs, although they must still support an adequate fund outcome. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0251] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0252]

## product_solution

- **In: clear customer value and urgent pain.** He favors category-defining niche products with tangible customer value and would rather see urgent demand for an imperfect product than elegant technology without revenue. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0220] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0187]

- **Inferred diligence question:** are founders repeatedly speaking with users, listening to operational pain, and incorporating feedback? Phillips calls that behavior customer obsession, but these operating examples should not be treated as a universal formal gate. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0109] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0110]

- **For AI, mere adoption is table stakes.** He wants AI to unlock something previously unattainable in go-to-market, customer experience, or retention, and he favors vertical AI where sector focus signals deep expertise. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0132] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0246]

- **Out: generic AI wrappers and underlying LLM bets.** This is an express exclusion, grounded in his view that weak moats, rapid change, and commoditization create a race to the bottom. It coexists with enthusiasm for applied vertical AI. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0131] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0165]

- **Hardware and CPG are not excluded.** He will consider either when product quality and a distinctive right to win are credible, reflecting a generalist rather than software-only mandate. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0054] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0050]

## traction_growth

- **Reported strike zone: usually post-revenue seed.** Repeated statements place the preferred revenue range near $500,000–$2 million, with realized revenue preferred to annualized ARR; some earlier or broader statements extend the upper end to $3 million. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0053] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0075] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0129]

- **Evolution matters.** Fund I included pre-seed and pre-revenue exposure, while Phillips later described Fund II as overwhelmingly or exclusively post-revenue under then-current conditions. Idea-on-a-napkin opportunities may merit conversation but not institutional capital. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0069] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0124] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0227]

- **Exception: strong contracted demand.** Signed hospital contracts provided meaningful validation for NQS before revenue and before product launch; Phillips explicitly treated pre-revenue status as a risk to underwrite rather than an automatic prohibition. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0168] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0180] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0202]

- **Growth must be repeatable without the owner.** He examines financial quality, variables that could impair continued results, and whether performance survives beyond the owner’s personal labor and relationships. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0018] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0020]

- **Fundraising should accelerate demonstrated growth, not substitute for it.** Phillips says raising capital should be an input into a business already experiencing significant growth. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0226]

## business_model_economics

- **Core preference: sustainable, durable, profitable growth.** Phillips rejects growth at all costs and favors companies that can use a moderately sized seed round to reach cash-flow breakeven, then choose between further financing and self-funded growth. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0014] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0049]

- **In: sound unit economics, financial visibility, and a path to profit.** He expects a financial model at every stage and is uncomfortable when management does not track assumptions and controllable metrics. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0174] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0175] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0218]

- **In: financing optionality.** One or two more rounds should be an option, not an assumption. He favors preserving founder ownership and warns founders not to sell equity unless capital is necessary for their desired scale. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0309] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0223] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0229]

- **Target company archetype:** the middle ground between a lifestyle business and a capital-intensive hypergrowth startup—ambitious founders, scalable economics, and meaningful growth without $10–20 million seed “rocket fuel.” [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0062] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0063]

- **He acknowledges the tradeoff.** Taking less capital may cap maximum company upside, but the fund seeks a higher success rate and attractive moderate exits rather than depending entirely on rare unicorns. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0060] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0061]

- **AI economics must create measurable value.** Phillips asks about return on each token used; interest in AI does not excuse undisciplined spending. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0255]

## deal_terms_valuation

- **Price is central.** Phillips calls 11 Tribes very price-sensitive and says inflated rounds can remove prospective upside and create future financing risk. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0046] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0254] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0286]

- **Entry ranges evolved with fund and market.** Earlier accounts referenced approximately $8–10 million average pre-money entries; an early Fund II fundraising description sought a sub-$8 million pre-money cap. Other Fund II remarks cited $10–15 million pre-money, $10–15 million post-money, $15–20 million post-money, or $15–25 million post-money. These are source-specific snapshots, not one timeless hard cap. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0183] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0069] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0052] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0090] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0248] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0160]

- **Check sizes also evolved.** Fund I sources describe roughly $250,000–$300,000 seed checks; Fund II targets $1 million–$1.5 million across 25–30 companies, with approximately $2–3 million total rounds. [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0065] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0127] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0128]

- **Ownership and governance evolved.** Earlier statements described minority positions and roughly 5–7% ownership plus pro rata rights; a later 2026 first-party account says the firm leads virtually every round and uses board seats to influence growth philosophy. NQS was an expressly unusual co-investment that 11 Tribes did not lead. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0104] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0310] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0145]

- **Underwriting targets, not promises:** he repeatedly references a credible path to roughly 10x, with modeled exits variously around $50 million, $100–150 million, $100–300 million, or a broader $50–250 million range depending on source and entry price. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0099] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0219] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0250] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0161]

- **No fixed buyer is required at seed.** He thinks about outcome structure from investment inception, but does not require an identified acquirer during diligence. He does value sustainable profitability and a broad plausible buyer universe. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0012] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0105] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0162]

## timing

- **Seed and Series A are the stated stage focus.** The firm is relationship-driven at those stages, with trusted relationships also central to sourcing and syndication. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0209] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0166] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0115]

- **Geographic timing thesis:** non-coastal North American ecosystems can offer strong operators at substantially lower valuations than Silicon Valley. Phillips has described Nashville discounts as roughly 2–2.5x and Chattanooga discounts as 3–5x; these are his estimates, not universal market measurements. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0047] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0048] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0164]

- **Thematic evolution:** 2021 commentary emphasized post-pandemic distributed work, virtual education, agtech, sustainability, and food traceability; later material increasingly emphasizes vertical AI, operator-founders, and niche enduring companies. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0195] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0201] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0192]

- **Market timing posture is disciplined rather than predictive.** He wrote that no one knows where markets are headed and advocated patient, opportunistic deployment, although separate updates called the then-current period an unusually attractive venture vintage. Those time-bound views should not be treated as permanent. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0272] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0301] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0333]

## investor_fit_constraints

- **Geography:** primarily domestic/North American, with emphasis on tier-two and tier-three, non-coastal markets; an early source reports mostly U.S. investments plus one Mexico investment, while later language is more explicitly North American and price-driven. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0047] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0025] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0163]

- **Sector:** broadly generalist and historically active across healthcare, financial services, agriculture, education, future of work, cybersecurity, data, food/agtech, CPG, and hardware. Sector concentration can still stop another investment even without a direct conflict. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0098] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0102] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0074]

- **Express exclusions:** vice industries such as alcohol and marijuana; generic AI wrappers and underlying LLM companies. Religious affiliation is not a screen: Phillips says the faith-inspired fund does not request statements of faith and invests in Christian and non-Christian founders. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0032] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0055] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0023]

- **Alignment can kill a deal.** Founders committed to heavily financed billion-dollar trajectories may not fit; disagreement with a lead investor’s underwriting or growth profile may also cause a walk-away. The firm’s partnership philosophy is reportedly shared late in diligence specifically to expose this mismatch. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0064] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0169] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0148]

- **Process:** an early source described a four-person investment committee requiring unanimity; a later source described three partners on the committee, suggesting a change or a difference in how membership was described; exact timing is not established. Typical diligence was reported at 40–50 hours with detailed memoranda and independent pre-meeting scoring. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0373] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0152] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0149] [ctx:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0150]

- **Investor value-add fit:** the best match wants a hands-on partner, direct feedback, experienced subject-matter support, founder-development resources, and—in the later model—a lead investor with board influence. A founder wanting capital without philosophical engagement is a weaker fit. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0199] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0216] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0280] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0310]

- **Tentative inferred diligence checklist:** Why is this founder uniquely suited to the problem? What truly motivates them? Can demand, earnings, and customer acquisition persist beyond the owner? What distribution or integration moat survives copyable code? Can a properly sized round reach breakeven? Do entry price and likely buyer universe support the fund’s return? Can the team accept hard feedback and remain aligned with a durable-growth path? This synthesis combines explicit questions with operating guidance and is not a published mandatory scorecard. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0033] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0018] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0245] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0049] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0099] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0177]

## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

- **Dual-outcome capitalism:** Phillips explicitly rejects financial maximization as the only objective and seeks optimization across financial, human, social, and environmental capital, with founder outcomes treated as core rather than collateral. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0004] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0269] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0270]

- **Faith-inspired but not faith-exclusive:** Christian ideas visibly shape his language about stewardship, human flourishing, servant leadership, and purpose, yet he expressly declines religious affiliation as an eligibility requirement. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0037] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0363] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0364]

- **Care is deliberately demanding.** His model combines psychological safety with early, direct confrontation, commitments, timelines, and accountability; founder care is not presented as avoiding hard decisions. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0280] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0282] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0284]

- **Fund design mirrors company design.** He intentionally keeps the fund comparatively small and agile so moderate financings and sub-unicorn exits can still generate attractive returns and the firm can remain operationally involved. [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0268] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0288] [ev:mark-phillips-11-tribes-ventures-0305]
