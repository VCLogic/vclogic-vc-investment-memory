# Recurring themes

- **Explicit — Customer behavior is the core validation mechanism.** Revenue matters, but repeat or expansion purchasing is especially important because Hyatt treats it as evidence that reduces risk and supports valuation. [ev:michael-hyatt-0012] [ev:michael-hyatt-0014]
- **Explicit — Product creation is not equivalent to company validation.** An MVP and a talented team without clients may be interesting, but Hyatt does not regard those elements alone as proof that a company exists in an investable commercial sense. [ev:michael-hyatt-0013]
- **Explicit — Founder relationship quality is part of the investment itself.** Hyatt wants to back entrepreneurs he likes and believes in, with whom he expects to sustain a decade-long working relationship; brilliance does not compensate for unacceptable character. [ev:michael-hyatt-0020] [ev:michael-hyatt-0021]
- **Explicit — Ambition should be consequential and aligned.** He seeks partners who want to build a large company, create employment, make a difference, and pursue something he regards as great. [ev:michael-hyatt-0023]
- **Explicit — B2B fits him better than capital-hungry consumer scaling.** He directly prefers B2B and describes many B2C models as vulnerable to enormous, continuing financing requirements. This is a personal preference and capability fit, not a universal sector prohibition. [ev:michael-hyatt-0015] [ev:michael-hyatt-0016]
- **Explicit — Active contribution is scarce and deliberately concentrated.** He describes leaning into only a few companies with board service, time, and advice, and may syndicate with others who can add help even when he could supply the round himself. [ev:michael-hyatt-0024] [ev:michael-hyatt-0025]
- **Explicit — There is no fund-driven need to transact.** Hyatt says he invests through a family office, does not seek deal flow, and can tolerate a very long interval without another deal. [ev:michael-hyatt-0026]
- **Inferred — Consistent small advances may matter more than a single dramatic event.** His operating lessons emphasize cumulative execution, hiring, and incremental revenue while warning that one large contract is not a complete success model. [ev:michael-hyatt-0002] [ev:michael-hyatt-0003] [ev:michael-hyatt-0004]
- **Inferred — Focus may be an execution advantage.** He repeatedly advises doing one thing well and associates excessive simultaneous priorities with weaker performance, but does not state this as a hard investment screen. [ev:michael-hyatt-0005] [ev:michael-hyatt-0006]
- **Inferred — Capital efficiency and founder ownership may be valued.** His favorable account of bootstrapping reflects experience with conserving ownership before an exit, without establishing mandatory dilution or ownership limits. [ev:michael-hyatt-0010]
- **Inferred — Plans require an uncertainty discount.** Hyatt expects company-building to cost more and take longer than planned and is skeptical of precise hockey-stick projections. [ev:michael-hyatt-0018]
- **Inferred — Adverse periods can be favorable formation environments.** He argues that bad times may reduce competitive attention and give a new company room to develop. [ev:michael-hyatt-0009]
- **Inferred — Historical technology optimism, not a current mandate.** Across undated remarks, Hyatt described exponential technology progress, cost compression, an early-internet-like environment, and AI as still being very early. These statements show historical enthusiasm but do not establish a current sector allocation or forecast. [ev:michael-hyatt-0007] [ev:michael-hyatt-0030] [ev:michael-hyatt-0031] [ev:michael-hyatt-0032]
