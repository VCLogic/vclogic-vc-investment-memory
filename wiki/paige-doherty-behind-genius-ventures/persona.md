# Paige Doherty — Investment Memory

This memory covers the resolved Paige Finn Doherty affiliated with Behind Genius Ventures (BGV), excluding same-name individuals. It synthesizes her own statements, jointly authored firm frameworks, and source-reported relationships. Direct quotations and attributed views are treated as evidence; contextual claims remain source-reported rather than independently verified. Several pages duplicate the same underlying articles, some publication dates are absent, and six podcast audio files lacked transcripts. Portfolio references generally establish BGV or fund relationships—not personal ownership—and apparently current facts should be read as of their source dates.

## founder_team

Doherty’s central founder archetype is the “technical storyteller”: a founder combining technical or domain depth, strong communication, visionary leadership, adaptability, and execution velocity. Her explicit three-axis formulation asks whether the founder is a good storyteller, has a strong mission, and executes quickly once the right priority is known. Storytelling means more than presentation polish: it includes listening, understanding context, translating technical ideas for customers, investors, and employees, and moving fluidly between product, team, vision, and industry-landscape levels. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0094] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0157] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0168] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0170]

Her strongest founder-market-fit signal is unusually specific, lived knowledge of the problem. She looks for unique domain expertise, a credible explanation of why this is the right team, and details that could only come from direct experience. Maneva’s founders combined factory and manual-labor exposure with AI, sensors, computer vision, and embedded-systems expertise; Hostie paired restaurant operations with engineering depth; and Intramotev’s team demonstrated detailed rail knowledge. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0016] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0051] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0183] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0136] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0138] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0133]

Landscape understanding is especially diagnostic. Doherty argues that it separates good founders from great ones because even a good product and team can be blindsided without awareness of industry shifts. Deep landscape knowledge also signals authentic passion, particularly in obscure fields, and the strongest founders treat the company as a career-spanning or generational endeavor. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0172] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0173] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0174] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0175]

She also values curiosity, grit, mission commitment, operating acumen, work ethic, talent magnetism, and the capacity to lead a generation-defining company. Reference calls on MoldCo were used to test execution speed, communication, and operating ability; later experience reinforced her regard for directness, drive, research rigor, and talent attraction. Firm negotiation is another favorable signal because she sees it as suggestive of how the founder will negotiate with customers. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0171] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0031] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0112] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0114] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0115] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0116] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0096]

Communication quality affects both selection and the working relationship. Concise, personalized outreach with a grounded problem/solution description, a concrete customer-ROI example, and a compelling founder-right-to-win case performs well. Obvious AI-generated outreach is only a “beige flag,” not a categorical rejection, because communication is itself an important business capability. Failure to ask strong questions is another mild caution. Warm introductions are considered the strongest access channel, but the evidence does not say cold outreach is disqualifying. Inferred decision questions, grounded in her stated criteria, are: What direct experiences generated the founder’s insight? Why is this team uniquely equipped to solve the problem? Can the founders explain the product, organization, long-term vision, and changing landscape with equal clarity? What did references reveal about execution velocity, work ethic, and talent attraction? These are inferred applications of her framework, not a verbatim mandatory checklist. ## market_opportunity

She seeks overlooked markets that can still produce venture-scale outcomes. Her explicit sizing thought experiment asks how a business can reach roughly $500 million to $1 billion in revenue while capturing less than 10% of its market; the point is execution margin for error, not a universal numeric prerequisite. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0007] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0008] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0018] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0019]

Her “clipboard company” framework favors neglected, antiquated, operationally heavy industries with relatively few technology competitors, provided a focused entry niche can broaden into venture-scale returns. Examples include manufacturing, metals servicing, rail freight, perishable logistics, restaurants, seaweed farming and Earth observation, regenerative agriculture, and complex health journeys. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0118] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0232] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0233] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0234] [ctx:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0164] [ctx:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0165]

She responds to concrete evidence of costly dysfunction: slow quoting, delivery delays, manual setup and paper workflows in metals; labor, downtime, and error costs in factories; missed reservations and labor shortages in restaurants; and recurring API-demonstration friction observed firsthand at WorkOS. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0067] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0135] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0083] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0085]

Specialization can expand markets previously considered too niche by making systems reliable enough to perform real work and draw from labor budgets. Six-figure annual contracts for agent access are described as strong market validation, not a requirement for every investment; Coast shows that she can invest pre-revenue when firsthand problem conviction is strong. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0161] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0162] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0163] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0088]

Inferred questions are: Is the pain frequent, expensive, and grounded in observed workflows? Can the initial niche expand without demanding implausible share? Is the market overlooked because it is genuinely neglected, or merely structurally unattractive? These synthesize her market-sizing and clipboard frameworks and should not be treated as quoted gates. ## competition_defensibility

Doherty prefers specificity over generic competitive claims. She asks whether founders have evaluated alternatives and spoken with relevant teams, valuing granular field knowledge that cannot be fabricated through superficial AI-assisted research. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0021]

Defensibility varies by business. Nox’s recurring order data can compound into a proprietary operational-data moat; Maneva’s edge-based training enables real-time factory action; Coldcart’s specialization distinguishes it from both asset-heavy operators and general logistics software; Hostie’s operator-led hospitality focus differentiates it from horizontal solutions; and Coast’s architecture and self-serve motion addressed a known API-company need. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0068] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0040] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0060] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0139] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0087]

She is wary of crowded verticals and the possibility that well-funded incumbents, major technology companies, or research institutions can enter. In AI, reliance on non-proprietary models may weaken defensibility, while application-specific fine-tuning, integration, deployment, proprietary data, trust, and domain expertise can create advantage. Investor enthusiasm or an oversubscribed round is noteworthy but explicitly not sufficient evidence of future success. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0211] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0215] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0150] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0207] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0208]

Inferred questions are: What compounds with usage? What prevents an incumbent from replicating the offering? Is specialization creating reliability, data, distribution, or workflow lock-in? What evidence shows customers choose this product over named alternatives? These are tentative deductions from her diligence examples. ## product_solution

She favors grounded solutions that visibly improve work. For application-layer AI, targeted products should solve recognizable needs without requiring users to become AI experts; agent success should be measured through time saved, reliable task completion, and reduced human intervention. Specialized AI is best suited to “kind” environments with clear rules, repetition, and immediate feedback. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0152] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0153] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0160] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0166]

The clipboard framework is human-centered rather than purely substitutive: technology should enhance and preserve valuable expertise, respect existing industry knowledge, and avoid oversimplifying complex systems. This creates a productive tension with her interest in autonomous agents: automation is attractive when it removes repetitive friction, but experienced humans remain important in complex traditional domains. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0235] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0236] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0238] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0239]

Technical diligence tests whether the product is genuinely innovative, can do what it claims, and rests on a sustainable model. Relevant AI risks include model constraints, API costs, compute requirements, third-party-model dependence, profitability, explainability, trust, and adoption. MoldCo illustrates willingness to conduct extensive scientific review, read a 44-page paper, pursue follow-up questions, and perform independent research. ## traction_growth

Traction is stage-sensitive. She has invested pre-revenue when she had strong firsthand conviction in the problem, but elsewhere values concrete customer ROI, high-ticket orders, switching behavior, measurable operational improvements, and realistic financial assumptions. Strong validation should not be misread as a universal revenue prerequisite. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0088] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0015] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0132] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0140] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0141] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0217]

Expansion logic matters. Maneva’s initial factory agent is expected to demonstrate savings in labor, errors, and downtime, making subsequent agents easier to sell. Nox was reported to be winning against large incumbents through instant quoting and frictionless ordering. Coast was later reported to be scaling profitably without another financing, while Hostie was reported to have grown revenue tenfold in the year after BGV’s seed investment; these are source-reported, company-specific outcomes rather than universal benchmarks. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0190] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0070] [ctx:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0224] [ctx:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0198]

Her later exit research stresses endurance: the studied billion-dollar outcomes generally took 10–12 years, with rapid exits under five years the exception. The same research says revenue at listing varies materially by business type, so it should not be converted into a single maturity gate for early-stage selection. ## business_model_economics

Doherty repeatedly favors asset-light or capital-efficient structures when they improve adaptability and economics. Coldcart’s software-centered model avoids owning physical infrastructure; Maneva integrates into existing factories rather than depending on hardware-heavy replacement; and Intramotev retrofits existing railcars instead of developing new ones from scratch. These examples show a preference, not a blanket rejection of hardware. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0043] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0044] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0036] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0130]

She is explicitly willing to invest in hardware and believes specialized vertical hardware can create meaningful returns. The relevant distinction appears to be whether capital intensity produces defensible customer value and a plausible path to profitability; rising capital needs or uncertain future value creation can later justify taking some secondary liquidity. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0266] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0267] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0209] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0282]

She tests projections for realistic assumptions and favors models where demonstrated ROI supports expansion. In creator businesses, she has highlighted results and deep data; in marketplaces and ecosystems, later research identified network effects as leverage; and in marketing technology it identified first-party data supporting engagement and lifetime value. The latter observations come from exit research and should not be treated as exclusive fund mandates. ## deal_terms_valuation

BGV focuses primarily on pre-seed and seed investments. In a later interview Doherty reported average checks of $250,000–$500,000, although The Pitch separately reported $150,000–$250,000; the difference may reflect source timing, venue, or product and should remain unresolved. BGV co-invests rather than leading, but may introduce a lead, join just after one, bring in other co-investors, or invest between rounds. ## timing

Timing has several layers. At company formation, she looks for technology and behavior shifts that open overlooked markets: applied-AI advances, commoditizing foundation models, application-layer value creation, agent proliferation, domestic manufacturing resiliency, and adoption of human-technology collaboration. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0091] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0149] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0154] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0071] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0240]

Her sector emphasis evolved. In 2021 she described BGV as investing across future of work and play, including developer tools, creators, media, gaming, entertainment, and wellness. By the 2024 Fund II launch and subsequent writing, technical storytellers and clipboard companies—especially vertical and applied AI in overlooked industries—became much more explicit. This looks like sharpening and expansion rather than a documented repudiation of the earlier generalist mandate; later consumer investing retained a “touch grass, make friends” preference for offline activity and social connection. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0101] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0102] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0103] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0167] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0097] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0124]

For fundraising, tightly synchronized conversations can concentrate investor attention; this is advice to founders, not a selection gate. For fund liquidity, Doherty sees secondaries as a structural development and favors disciplined, conviction-weighted timing. She warns that premature sales, especially in less-liquid companies, may materially reduce returns; QSBS’s five-year threshold may begin the discussion but does not mechanically determine a sale. ## investor_fit_constraints

The clearest mandate is concentrated pre-seed and seed investing in the United States and Canada, centered on technical storytellers and increasingly on applied AI, frontier technology, and overlooked operational industries. BGV’s later reported portfolio exceeded 50 companies across a $5 million Fund I and an $8.9 million Fund II, while the homepage separately announced a $15 million Fund 2 without a supplied date; these figures should not be reconciled without more evidence. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0178] [ctx:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0016] [ctx:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0170] [ctx:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0174]

Strong fit: technically or operationally credible founders who communicate with clarity; painful and specific workflows; overlooked markets with broadening potential; application-layer AI, vertical software, selective hardware, and products that measurably save time or money while respecting domain expertise. Weaker fit or caution: generic founder narratives, conspicuously automated communication, shallow industry understanding, crowded markets vulnerable to incumbent entry, technically impressive AI with poor economics or third-party dependency, and projections whose assumptions are not grounded in reality. These are risk signals rather than universal automatic rejections. Reported platform support includes pricing research, storytelling, introductions, and partnerships that can reduce operating costs. ## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

Doherty’s investment practice is unusually intertwined with writing, community, and explicit framework-building. She says published case studies improve the decision framework itself, prioritizes who acts on the work over raw impressions, and has experimented with applying ideas learned through painting to investment decisions. These are process characteristics, not separate sector mandates. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0001] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0145] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0148]

Fund and personal investing must be kept distinct. She said angel and syndicate decisions are more personal, whereas a fund requires thesis, strategy, stage, and portfolio fit; she considered those constraints enriching. Pallet was her first syndicate deal, while most later “we invested” statements concern BGV or a named fund. [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0105] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0106] [ev:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0107] [ctx:paige-doherty-behind-genius-ventures-0119]

A tentative synthesized decision policy is therefore: start with founder authenticity, technical or domain depth, communication, mission, and execution; establish a real and costly problem; test whether an overlooked entry wedge can become venture-scale; examine differentiation, incumbent response, feasibility, economics, and realistic projections; then assess stage, terms, ownership, cap table, and long-term working compatibility. This synthesis is inferred from her recurring criteria and should not be treated as a mechanical scorecard.
