# Sheel Mohnot — Investment Memory

This memory concerns the resolved Sheel Mohnot associated with Better Tomorrow Ventures (BTV), identified by BTV’s official site as co-founder and general partner. It excludes same-name people. It synthesizes verified quotations and attributed reporting; context claims remain source-reported rather than independently verified. Several sources repeat the same interviews, many lack publication dates, some podcast media had no transcript, and one profile expressly says it is AI-generated. Current-sounding statements therefore apply only as of their source, and differences in fund size, check range, or sector description may reflect different dates, vehicles, or source quality. [ctx:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0216] [ctx:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0132]

## founder_team

The team is the center of Mohnot’s seed-stage decision. He says the number-one consideration at seed is the team because companies and products pivot while the team persists; elsewhere he similarly calls the entrepreneur the most important variable at his stage. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0017] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0018] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0029] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0034]

His strongest positive signals are leadership capacity, execution, and communication. He looks for people who could eventually lead hundreds of employees, while acknowledging that this assessment sometimes rests on an unquantifiable gut impression. He asks whether founders can do the job, break through walls, and maintain healthy team dynamics. Speed of iteration and tenacity are explicitly critical: founders should try things rapidly and persist through obstacles. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0001] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0002] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0019] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0007]

A CEO should clearly articulate the vision and why the company will win. Mohnot treats storytelling as operationally consequential because the CEO must recruit employees, investors, and customers into that vision. Authenticity matters too: he says communication must come from a real place and that ghostwriting is detectable. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0021] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0022] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0028] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0087]

He ideally wants relevant domain knowledge plus business and technical capabilities on the founding team, though “ideally” should not be converted into a mandatory composition rule. His Flexport rationale illustrates the pattern: Ryan Petersen’s prior import businesses and scrappiness supported founder-market fit, while the combination of a large idea and an outstanding founder justified the investment. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0020] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0058] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0059]

Mohnot explicitly gravitates toward immigrants and people who have lived abroad, associating migration and exposure to struggle with boldness and entrepreneurial performance. He finds relocation to pursue an opportunity compelling. These are preferences, not universal gates. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0094] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0095] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0096]

Inferred, not a stated investment prerequisite: his operating advice favors founders willing to risk failure, embarrassment, and repeated experimentation. The inference is consistent with his explicit emphasis on iteration and tenacity but should not be elevated into a separate rejection rule. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0006] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0098]

Decision policy: lean In when an early team combines fast learning, resourceful execution, credible domain insight, complementary skills, constructive dynamics, and a CEO capable of recruiting stakeholders around a clear vision. Lean Out when the team appears afraid to test, or cannot explain why it wins. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0007] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0020] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0028]

## market_opportunity

Mohnot’s core market premise is that financial services represent roughly one-fifth of global GDP yet remain insufficiently digitized. Persistent human intervention and even fax-based processes demonstrate room for greater convenience and digitization. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0011] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0012] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0048] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0092] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0093]

He seeks companies capable of massive outcomes, reflecting venture’s asymmetric return structure: occasional large winners can drive the record. Inferred from his FeeFighters retrospective, a good standalone business may still be unsuitable for venture if the attainable outcome is too limited. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0003] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0026] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0055]

The opportunity test is not merely top-down size. His Flexport thesis paired a huge global-trade market with a visibly broken import/export process. Accounting is another concrete wedge: labor shortages force firms to reject customers, and Mohnot expects the shortage to worsen, creating room for technology and AI. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0057] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0014] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0050]

His market map has evolved. He initially underestimated prediction markets but later described them as a massive and fast-growing sector. He also reported increasing interest in consumer technology as AI improves ad targeting, while keeping prediction markets a major interest. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0040] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0042]

Decision policy: lean In on a large or expanding market where a concrete workflow is broken, expensive, manual, or supply-constrained and technology can unlock previously underserved demand. Ask: Is the market capable of an outlier outcome? What observable pain validates it? Is the opportunity genuinely under-digitized, or merely receiving cyclical funding attention? [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0009] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0015] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0063]

## competition_defensibility

Mohnot does not equate technical complexity with defensibility. Passing on Patreon because its payment functionality seemed easy to build became a lesson that apparent simplicity can obscure company potential. His Robinhood miss similarly taught him that superior execution, timing, mobile UX, and access for a new investor class can matter more than superficial product similarity. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0025] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0023]

Competitive density can still change the appropriate stage. BTV skipped “Plaid for payroll” when many similar companies appeared together, even though several later succeeded. In compliance software, uncertainty about whether the market could support multiple vendors made Mohnot consider Series A or later more sensible, when a likely winner would be clearer. These examples show caution, not an absolute ban on crowded categories. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0060] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0061]

Specialization is part of BTV’s own competitive advantage. Mohnot argues that specialist accelerators can overcome the adverse-selection problem faced by generalists competing with YC, and that a fintech-concentrated portfolio compounds reusable learning. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0067] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0071]

Inferred product defensibility: API infrastructure can become durable when customers begin building on it while small and remain as they scale. For embedded products, compliance can function as a superpower when executed correctly. Neither statement establishes a universal moat requirement. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0056] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0051]

Decision policy: assess whether execution, distribution, UX, embedded workflow position, compliance, or customer expansion can create advantage even when the code looks replicable. In crowded categories, ask whether the market supports several winners and whether seed-stage evidence can distinguish them; when neither is clear, waiting may be rational. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0023] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0025] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0061]

## product_solution

The broad product thesis is “everything is fintech.” Mohnot evaluates software through the possibility that financial services become integral to the product and, for some vertical SaaS companies, eventually generate most revenue. Primary operating software for a small business has a natural position from which to manage that customer’s finances. B2B marketplaces with meaningful fintech tendencies are also in scope. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0033] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0036] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0037] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0038] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0047]

Embedded fintech is a strong explicit theme: he expects SMB platforms such as Toast- or Shopify-like products to be increasingly driven by financial services and wants to back companies that enable that transition. When software companies add financial products, he believes specialized infrastructure partners often make the most sense. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0049] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0039]

AI expands the solution set. Mohnot identifies manual underwriting, compliance, fraud prevention, and customer service as workflows AI can perform more efficiently and cheaply. He also argues that companies need not sell AI products to benefit from AI’s speed, leanness, and scalability. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0015] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0016] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0031]

Inferred diligence: founders should show how the product changes cost, speed, convenience, or access in a real workflow, and whether a specialized partner is preferable to building regulated infrastructure internally. For first-time fintech founders, Mohnot warns that bank integrations and launches usually take much longer than expected; this is a financing and execution risk, not an automatic refusal to invest. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0039] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0052]

## traction_growth

At the earliest stages, Mohnot does not make a fixed traction threshold central; the team remains primary because product and technology may pivot. Nevertheless, his missed-investment reflections show what strong validation can look like: Robinhood combined timing and UX to bring a new customer population online, while Chime persuaded consumers to undertake the high-friction act of moving direct deposit. These are adoption signals, not mandatory prerequisites for every seed deal. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0034] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0023] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0024]

He is willing to revise judgments as evidence changes. An unnamed portfolio company was reportedly written down after stagnant revenue and limited runway, then grew profitably from $1 million to $6 million of revenue during 2024. The example cautions against treating a temporary plateau as irreversible while still taking runway seriously. [ctx:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0117]

Inferred questions: Has the product induced meaningful new behavior or removed enough friction to make customers switch? Is growth attributable to product execution and timing rather than a financing bubble? Can the team iterate quickly when the first direction fails? [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0023] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0024] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0007] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0013]

## business_model_economics

Mohnot distinguishes viable businesses from venture-scale businesses. FeeFighters’ original model could have been a good non-venture business, while its later gateway direction offered greater upside. He also sees attractive expansion properties in APIs whose customers start small and remain as they grow. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0055] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0056]

Embedded finance can reshape vertical-software economics because the financial layer may become the majority of revenue at scale. AI can further improve economics by replacing or augmenting costly manual work. Source reporting also characterizes a newer BTV sub-thesis as “services as software,” but that label comes from an AI-generated aggregator and should be treated as lower-confidence context than Mohnot’s direct workflow comments. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0036] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0016] [ctx:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0132] [ctx:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0138]

Inferred questions: Can the product capture a meaningful share of the financial activity already flowing through the customer’s operating system? Does automation materially reduce cost to serve? Do retention and expansion improve as customers scale? These are tentative deductions from his embedded-finance, AI, microfinance-cost, and API observations rather than published numeric gates. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0038] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0089] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0090] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0056]

## deal_terms_valuation

BTV describes itself as a lead investor at pre-seed and seed. Mohnot says leading rounds lets the firm control invested capital, obtain appropriate ownership, and build a fintech franchise. The official BTV site later stated a $500,000–$4 million lead-check sweet spot, while another report described Fund III checks of $500,000–$3.5 million; these should be treated as source-date ranges, not permanent personal limits. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0046] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0073] [ctx:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0217] [ctx:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0084]

The Mint has distinct standardized terms: Mohnot described investing $500,000 for 10% plus three months of close engagement. Those program terms should not be generalized to ordinary BTV investments. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0068]

Price discipline strengthened after 2021. Mohnot says rapid follow-on rounds at inflated prices were excessive and likely harmful; in one case, limited progress did not justify an almost twentyfold markup in four months. He separates that financing excess from the enduring fintech opportunity. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0013] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0032] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0062] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0063]

Follow-ons are selective rather than automatic. Mohnot calls follow-on capacity important for a lead, but BTV may decline when the original hypotheses fail. He says the firm concentrates follow-on capital in roughly its top 20%, sometimes through Series B, because venture returns are driven by outliers. A reported one-for-one reserve—one follow-on dollar per initial dollar—provides context but may be vehicle-specific. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0078] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0079] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0080] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0081] [ctx:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0131]

Decision policy: lean Out when price has detached sharply from operating progress or when a sound business lacks credible venture-scale upside. Ask what ownership the lead can obtain, what evidence justifies the current step-up, which hypotheses must hold for follow-on support, and whether future dilution preserves attractive fund returns. No universal valuation ceiling is supported. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0055] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0062] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0073] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0079]

## timing

Mohnot’s long-term conviction survived a volatile cycle. He describes 2021 as overhyped because low rates drove too much capital into fintech, followed by a severe 2022–2023 overcorrection; neither phase negated the opportunity to improve financial infrastructure. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0063]

Current timing is supported by unfinished digitization, widespread manual financial work, accounting labor shortages, and AI’s improving ability to automate those workflows. BTV invested heavily in AI after observing its portfolio effects. Smartphones were an earlier enabling shift, making underwriting and capital delivery faster for underserved borrowers. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0012] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0014] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0041] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0091]

Timing also matters at the company level: his Robinhood and Chime misses reinforced that mobile distribution, customer readiness, and execution can unlock a market even when an investor initially discounts the product. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0023] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0024]

## investor_fit_constraints

Best fit is a global, early-stage company within Mohnot’s broad fintech lens: core financial technology, embedded finance, financial infrastructure, vertical software with a substantial financial-services layer, or a B2B marketplace with fintech characteristics. BTV explicitly leads pre-seed and seed rounds, though Mohnot says the firm can be opportunistic and persuaded by founder conviction rather than rigidly thesis-driven. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0046] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0047] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0049] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0086]

Fit includes active partnership. Mohnot says private-market deals are won by being the best partner. BTV seeks to introduce founders to next-round investors, leverage bank relationships to shorten integration timelines, encourage portfolio-peer learning, and give even declined founders advice or a specific pass reason. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0035] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0043] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0044] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0053] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0070]

His operator history shapes that support model: he began investing to help founders avoid mistakes he had made while operating and fundraising. He believes founders are especially receptive to advice from people who have built companies themselves. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0045] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0030]

Boundaries are nuanced. The direct record supports fintech specialization but also a broad definition of fintech and increasing consumer interest. A Signal profile reports no interest in adtech, hardware, or social networks, but that is third-party database context and sits in tension with Mohnot’s newer stated consumer interest; treat it as a dated, lower-authority screening hint rather than a hard exclusion. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0036] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0042] [ctx:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0237]

Tentative inferred diligence checklist: Can this team lead at scale, iterate quickly, and recruit around a clear vision? Is the market both large and concretely broken? Does the product materially improve cost, speed, access, or convenience? Is there a credible path to defensibility and venture-scale economics? Are bank, compliance, and integration timelines realistic? Does the valuation match progress? These synthesize explicit views and operating advice; they are not published mandatory gates. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0001] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0007] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0028] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0015] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0052] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0062]

## Distinctive / doesn't-fit-the-taxonomy

Mohnot begins with a mission lens—whether a company is building a better world—and frames easier finance as part of a better future. This is a positive orientation, not evidence of a formal impact mandate or exclusion screen. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0027] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0005]

He is unusually explicit about founder experience after a pass: the goal is for every interaction to yield useful advice or a specific reason for declining. He also values concentrated specialist communities because lessons transfer among fintech companies. [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0044] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0070] [ev:sheel-mohnot-better-tomorrow-ventures-0071]

His framework contains productive tensions rather than a mechanical scorecard: strong fintech specialization coexists with opportunism; gut judgment coexists with concrete tests of execution; crowded categories can be skipped even when some become successful; and a written-down company can recover dramatically. The appropriate memory is therefore founder-first, thesis-informed, price-conscious, and updateable—not a set of universal numeric gates.
